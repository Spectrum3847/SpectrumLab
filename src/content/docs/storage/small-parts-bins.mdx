Robots are mostly small parts, and small parts are only useful if you can find them in under a minute. The lab's system has five layers — steel drawer cabinets for drivetrain parts, latching cases for fasteners, Stack-On boxes for kits, hanging organizers for belts, and 3D-printed grid bins inside everything. Every one of them is labeled. Learn the layers and you can find anything in the shop.

## Durham Steel Drawer Cabinets

The blue Durham MFG steel cabinets on the parts wall hold the drivetrain and mechanism stock — the parts you burn through every build season. Drawers are labeled by family, with dividers inside:

- **Bearings** — including a dedicated *Non-Radial Bearings* drawer
- **Hex shaft hardware** — 3/8" hex and 1/2" hex drawers with snap rings, spacers, bearings, and shaft collars
- **Fasteners** — 10-32 socket, 10-32 button head and countersunk
- **Mechanism stock** — MaxPlanetary, MaxSpline, pulleys and belts, chain, end caps and nut strips
- **Assembly consumables** — magnets, clecos, heat-set inserts, glue
- **Electrical and Kraken** motor spares
- **Rope, wire rope, and straps** — plus a First Aid drawer on the end

## HDX Cases in the Slot Rack

Latching HDX organizer cases live in plywood slot racks the team built for them ([Lab Upgrades, Jan 2024](https://www.spectrum3847.org/seasons/2024/#2024-01-05-lab-upgrades)) — each case slides into its own labeled slot like a library book. Cases are organized two ways:

- **By fastener size:** 10-32 socket head, 1/4-20, 8-32, 10-32 countersunk, wood screws — grab the whole case, work out of it, slide it back.
- **By system:** *THE Electrical Case*, *THE Kraken Case*, the *Swerve fastener box*, *Elevator Blocks*, even *GoPros*. When a subsystem needs its hardware, one case comes off the shelf.

## Belt Organizers

Timing belts hang in pocket organizers with a **button label per pocket** — tooth count and profile, 40T through 100T in 9 mm 5M. Read the label, take your belt, and never bury a belt in the wrong pocket: a mis-filed belt is a mis-built gearbox at 11 PM on a Saturday.

## The Grid-Bin & Labeling Standard

Inside drawers and on shelves, loose parts live in **3D-printed grid bins** printed from the team's parametric bin models — sawtooth racks for wrenches and screwdrivers, divided bins for taps and hex keys, trays for sockets. The standard is simple:

1. Every container gets a **printed Brother TZe label** on the front face — see [Label Makers](/storage/label-makers/) for the standard and how to print one.
2. Bin sizes come from the shared grid models, so bins tile cleanly into any drawer.
3. A bin holds **one thing**. "Miscellaneous" is not a thing.

Categories currently on the small-parts wall include 1/2" hex bearings, aluminum and nylon spacers, snap rings, shaft collars, standoffs, #10-32 and 1/4-20 bolts sorted by length, nuts and lock nuts, and washers.

## Putting Parts Away (Do It Right)

<SafetyWarning type="tip" title="The system is only as good as the last person who used it">
Finding a part takes 30 seconds when everything is where its label says. Every mis-filed handful of bolts costs the next ten people that time. Put parts away like you're the one looking for them tomorrow — because you are.
</SafetyWarning>

1. **Sort before you dump.** End-of-meeting bench sweepings do not go into bins by the handful. Identify each part, then file it.
2. **Match the label exactly.** A 10-32 button head does not live in the socket-head case because it was closer.
3. **Wrong-part sightings get fixed on the spot.** If you find a 1/4-20 in the 10-32 bin, move it now.
4. **Cases and boxes return to their slot** — latched, and facing label-out.
5. **Don't create unlabeled containers.** New bin or case? It gets a printed label before parts go in.

### Restock Flow

- Took the **last few** of something, or opened the last bag? That's a restock trigger — note it on the [Consumables Ledger](/inventory/consumables/) and tell a mentor.
- Restock goes **into the labeled bin**, not next to it in the shipping bag. Break bulk down when it arrives.
- New part number with no home? Print a bin, print a label, add it to the wall — don't squat in someone else's bin.
