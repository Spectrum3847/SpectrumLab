Most of a robot is fasteners, and most fastener problems are self-inflicted: wrong size driver, worn tool, or the wrong tool entirely. This page covers the hand drivers in the red toolbox. For powered driving, see the [Milwaukee M12 Ecosystem](/portable-power/m12-ecosystem/) — and remember an impact driver on a small screw is how threads die.

## Hex keys

FRC robots run on socket-head and button-head cap screws, which means hex keys are the tool you'll use most. The hex drawer has L-keys sorted by size into labeled bins, ball-end drivers, T-handles, and fold-up sets.

### Ball-end vs. straight

- **Straight (full-engagement) end**: contacts all six flats across the full depth of the socket. Use it for **final tightening and for breaking loose** any fastener that resists.
- **Ball end**: lets you drive at up to ~25° off-axis — great for reaching screws buried behind other mechanisms. But the ball only contacts the socket at a few small points.

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="When ball-ends strip fasteners">
A ball-end under high torque concentrates all the force on tiny contact patches — it will round out the screw socket (or snap the ball off inside it) exactly when the fastener is tightest. Use the ball end to *spin* screws in awkward spots, then finish or break loose with the straight end, fully seated. If a key feels loose in the socket, it's worn or the wrong size (metric in SAE, or vice versa) — stop and check.
</SafetyWarning>

### T-handles

T-handle hex drivers are faster to spin and easier on your hands than L-keys for repetitive work like swerve module assembly. Same rule applies: seat the tip fully before applying torque.

## Wrenches

- **Combination wrenches** (open end + box end): use the **box end** whenever you can — it grips all six corners and won't slip. The open end is for when you can't get the box over the nut.
- **Ratcheting wrenches**: box-end speed without removing the wrench every sixth of a turn. Ideal for nylock nuts, which drag the whole way down.
- **Adjustable wrenches**: a last resort. Snug the jaw fully and pull toward the fixed jaw. A loose adjustable rounds nuts.

## Sockets & nut drivers

The socket drawer has 1/4" and 3/8"-drive sets plus nut drivers. Sockets shine when you need real torque or repetition — pair a socket with a ratchet for frame bolts, or a nut driver for spinning nylocks while a hex key holds the screw. Six-point sockets grip better than twelve-point on stubborn fasteners.

## Screwdrivers

**Size the driver to the screw.** A #2 Phillips in a #1 screw (or the reverse) is the number-one cause of chewed-up screw heads. The tip should fill the recess completely with no wobble.

To prevent cam-out (the driver twisting up and out of a Phillips head):

1. Pick the exact right tip size.
2. Push **hard** along the screw axis — most of your effort goes into pushing, less into twisting.
3. Keep the driver perfectly in line with the screw.
4. If it slips once, stop. Re-check size and alignment before you strip it.

Precision screwdriver kits live in the same drawer bank for electronics-scale fasteners — see [Soldering & Test Bench](/electrical/soldering-test-bench/).

## FRC-common sizes

:::note[The two threads that run the robot]
Almost everything on an FRC robot is **#10-32** or **1/4"-20**. Memorize their drivers and you'll never dig through drawers mid-match-repair:

| Fastener | Driver |
|---|---|
| 10-32 socket head cap screw | 5/32" hex |
| 10-32 button head / countersunk | 1/8" hex |
| 10-32 nylock nut | 3/8" wrench or socket |
| 1/4-20 socket head cap screw | 3/16" hex |
| 1/4-20 button head | 5/32" hex |
| 1/4-20 nylock nut | 7/16" wrench or socket |
:::

## Ground rules

- Return every driver to its labeled bin — a missing 5/32" key at competition is a crisis.
- Retire rounded hex keys and chewed screwdriver tips; tell a mentor so they get replaced.
- Never extend a wrench with a pipe ("cheater bar") — if it won't move, you need penetrating oil, heat, or a mentor, not more leverage.
- Threadlocker (blue) on fasteners that see vibration — but never on nylon-insert nuts, which don't need it.
