Milwaukee handles the small cordless work; DeWalt covers the bigger battery tools — track saw, heat gun, wet/dry vacuum, and a cordless compressor — and the Quincy in the outdoor enclosure feeds the shop's fixed air lines. This page covers all of it, plus the rules that keep compressed air boring and safe.

## FLEXVOLT Track Saw — DCS520

The 60V track saw is how we break down full sheets of plywood and polycarbonate to straight, splinter-free, table-saw-quality edges — on the floor, without wrestling a 4×8 sheet onto a saw.

<MachineSOP
  ppe={['goggles', 'hearing', 'closed-shoes', 'tie-hair']}
  training="Mentor sign-off required before first solo use."
/>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Plunge saws kick backward">
Never pull the saw backward along the track with the blade in the work, and keep the anti-kickback knob engaged — it locks the saw against riding back up the track. The riving knife stays installed; it keeps the kerf from pinching the blade closed.
</SafetyWarning>

**Making a plunge cut:**

1. Support the sheet on sacrificial foam or scrap stringers so the offcut can't pinch the blade. Never cut a sheet bridged between two tables.
2. Set the depth: material thickness plus about 1/8–1/4" — no deeper.
3. Lay the track with its rubber grip strips on the cut line (the zero-clearance edge *is* the cut line). **Clamp the track** on slick sheets, short pieces, or any cut where a shifted track ruins the part.
4. Set the saw on the track behind the start of the cut, engage the anti-kickback knob, start the motor at full speed, then plunge and push steadily forward.
5. Release the plunge at the end, let the blade retract and stop, then lift.

Score cuts (a shallow 1–2 mm first pass) give chip-free edges on veneered plywood and polycarb.

## 20V Heat Gun — DCE530

The cordless heat gun does two very different jobs: shrinking heat-shrink tubing on wiring, and warming aluminum housings for bearing fits. Two temperature settings: roughly 550 °F on low and up to 990 °F on high, about 42 minutes on a 5Ah pack.

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="990 °F leaves no blister warning">
The nozzle stays burn-hot for minutes after the trigger releases. Set the gun down on its rear stand pointing up, never on its side, and keep it away from anything flammable — 990 °F is well past the ignition point of paper, solvents, and polycarb film.
</SafetyWarning>

- **Heat shrink:** low setting, keep the gun moving, rotate around the joint until the tube snugs down evenly. Adhesive-lined shrink on battery lugs needs a touch more dwell until adhesive beads at the ends — see [Wire Prep & Crimping](/electrical/wire-prep-crimping/).
- **Bearing fits:** warm the aluminum housing (not the bearing) evenly for a minute or two on high, and the bore grows enough for the bearing to drop or press in gently. Heat the part, not one spot — and handle with gloves after.
- Don't point it at 3D-printed parts, polycarb, or wire insulation you intend to keep; they deform far below max temperature.
- Lock-off the trigger and let it cool on the stand before it goes back in the drawer.

## 20V Wet/Dry Vacuum

The DeWalt cordless wet/dry vac (the shop unit is the DCV581H hybrid — same 2-gallon family as the DCV580) lives on a shelf under the workbench for exactly one reason: mess happens away from wall outlets. HEPA-rated washable filter, wet and dry pickup, 5 ft hose.

<LabGalleryImage src="dewalt-wet-dry-vacuum.jpg" alt="DeWalt DCV581H cordless wet/dry vacuum on a shelf under the workbench" caption="The cordless wet/dry vac stores under the workbench — grab it for chip and spill cleanup anywhere in the shop." />

- Use it for machine chip trays, bench cleanup, and water around the WAZER.
- **After wet pickup:** empty the tank, rinse it, and let the filter dry fully before the next dry job — a wet filter clogs into cardboard.
- The powder room has its own dedicated shop vac; keep this one out of powder overspray.
- Filter is washable — rinse when suction drops, dry completely, reinstall.

## Cordless Compressor — DCC2560

The 60V FLEXVOLT compressor is the portable air source: 2.5-gallon tank, 135 PSI max, about 1.2 SCFM at 90 PSI, oil-free, with a OneTurn regulator — 21.5 lb you can carry to a competition pit or the far end of the field build.

<LabGalleryImage src="dewalt-cordless-compressor.jpg" alt="DeWalt cordless air compressor marked 3847 with coiled yellow hose under a workbench" caption="The DeWalt cordless compressor, marked 3847, stored under the workbench with its hose coiled." />

- Right-sized for burst tools: the pneumatic rivet gun, blow-off nozzles, filling pneumatics off-site. It is **not** a continuous supply for sanders or grinders — the tank runs down and the duty cycle can't keep up.
- Dial the regulator to the tool's rated pressure, not the tank max.
- Drain the tank after use (condensate collects even on light days) and coil the hose before it goes back under the bench.

## Shop Air — Quincy QT-5

Fixed shop air comes from the **Quincy QT-5** (5 HP, 80-gallon, two-stage) in the outdoor chain-link enclosure next to the shop vacuum dust collector, feeding the house lines through an air dryer. The compressor itself runs to **130+ PSI** in the tank; wall regulators hold the working lines at **120 PSI**. It is also the assist-air supply for both [fiber lasers](/lasers/fiber-laser-cutting/) — compressed air is the only assist gas we run.

<LabGalleryImage src="quincy-shop-compressor.jpg" alt="Quincy QT-5 vertical shop compressor with air dryer in the outdoor equipment enclosure" caption="The Quincy QT-5 and its air dryer live in the outdoor enclosure — the dryer knocks moisture out before air reaches the house lines." />

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Compressed air is not a toy">
Never point compressed air at skin, eyes, or clothing — 120 PSI drives debris and can inject air through skin. Blow-off means parts and machines, at arm's length, goggles on.
</SafetyWarning>

**Air line discipline** (this is on the [daily maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/) — it isn't optional):

- **Opening:** turn the compressor on and verify the regulator reads 120 PSI before anyone connects a tool.
- **Closing:** drain the air from the pneumatic lines and switch the compressor off. Lines left charged overnight sweat moisture into every fitting.
- The dryer removes most moisture, but water in a line kills air tools and rusts fittings — if a drop ever spits from a coupler, tell a mentor so the dryer and drains get checked.
- Hoses coiled and hung after use; a hose across the floor is a trip line.
- Depressurize before disconnecting any tool — crack the coupler slowly.

## Pneumatic Rivet Gun

The air rivet gun runs off house air (or the DeWalt compressor off-site) and out-paces the M12 rivet tool on long runs — bumper rails, full sheet-metal panels, batches of 3/16" stainless.

- Rivet sizing, grip range, and hole sizes are identical to the cordless tool — the [M12 Riveting page](/portable-power/m12-riveting/) chart applies verbatim.
- **Disconnect the air line** before swapping nose pieces or clearing a jammed mandrel. A connected air tool is a live tool.
- Empty its mandrel catcher just like the M12 tool — spent mandrels never go on the floor.
- If it feels sluggish, a couple drops of air-tool oil in the inlet fixes it; if that doesn't, flag it.

One neighbor worth knowing: the folding tapping arm mounted by the Evolution chop saw is **servo-electric, not pneumatic** — it needs no air line. It has its own SOP: [Electric Tapping Machine](/cutting/tapping-machine/).

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Track saw: depth reset to zero, saw and track cased, offcuts sorted to the scrap rack.
- Heat gun: cooled on its stand, then battery out and back in the drawer.
- Vacuum: tank emptied (always after wet pickup), filter checked.
- Compressors: DeWalt tank drained; Quincy off and lines drained at close per the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).
- DeWalt batteries follow the same rules as M12 packs — dead ones to the [charging bench](/portable-power/m12-ecosystem/#batteries--charging-discipline), never stored in tools.
