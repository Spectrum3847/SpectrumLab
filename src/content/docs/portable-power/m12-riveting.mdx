With the 6" long nose extension it also reaches places a hand riveter never will. ## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Mandrels are needles">
Spent <Term id="mandrel">mandrels</Term> are hardened steel spikes. Keep the collection cup on the tool, empty it into the metal recycle when it's half full, and never let mandrels hit the floor — they end up in shoes, tires, and knees.
</SafetyWarning>

- Safety goggles on. Mandrels occasionally snap early and let go with energy.
- Fingers clear of the nose piece — the tool pulls with over a thousand pounds of force.
- Remove the battery before swapping nose pieces or opening the jaw housing.

## Rivet Sizing

A blind rivet is specified by **diameter** and **<Term id="grip-range">grip range</Term>**. Get both right or the joint is decoration.

**1. Match the hole to the rivet diameter:**

| Rivet dia. | Drill size | Hole dia. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3/32" | #41 | 0.096" |
| 1/8" | #30 | 0.129" |
| 5/32" | #20 | 0.161" |
| 3/16" | #11 | 0.191" |

1/8" is the team default for sheet aluminum and polycarb; 3/16" for structural joints. A sloppy, oversized hole means the rivet body expands into air instead of clamping the stack.

**2. Match the grip range to the material stack.** Measure the **total thickness** of everything the rivet passes through. That number must fall inside the rivet's grip range — printed on the box and on the size chart in the drawer.

- Stack **thicker** than grip range: the rivet can't form a shop head. It holds until it doesn't.
- Stack much **thinner** than grip range: the body buckles inside the hole instead of clamping.

## Aluminum vs. Stainless Rivets

- **Aluminum rivets** are the default: aluminum sheet, polycarb, gussets, anything non-structural or lightly loaded. Easy on the tool, easy to drill out.
- **Stainless rivets** for high-shear structural joints and steel parts. The tool pulls up to 3/16" stainless, but each one works the jaws harder — expect to clean the jaws sooner, and mind the mandrel stubs, which snap off sharp.
- Don't mix metals carelessly: a stainless rivet in a thin aluminum sheet will oval the hole under load before the rivet gives.

Bulk rivet stock lives on the small-parts wall and in the labeled Stack-On boxes.

## Nose Piece Swap

The tool carries one nose piece installed and the others stowed. The nose piece **must match the rivet diameter** — a 3/16" mandrel will not seat in a 1/8" nose.

1. Battery out.
2. Unscrew the installed nose piece with the wrench flats.
3. Thread on the size you need and snug it — hand-tight plus a light wrench nip. A loose nose piece backs off mid-job.
4. Stow the old one back in the drawer holder, not your pocket.

Retention-style nose pieces (48-54-0100) grip the mandrel so the rivet stays put at any angle — worth installing before overhead or sideways riveting.

## Operating Instructions

1. Drill all holes to the chart size and deburr both faces — burrs hold the sheets apart and the rivet clamps the gap, not the metal.
2. Align the parts and hold the pattern with **clecos** (Durham drawer, "Magnets/Clecos" label) on anything more than two holes. Rivet, then leapfrog the clecos.
3. Insert the rivet mandrel into the nose piece until it seats.
4. Put the rivet body through the hole and hold the tool **square to the surface, nose pressed flush**. A tilted tool makes a tilted, weak shop head.
5. Squeeze the trigger and hold until the mandrel snaps — the tool pulls, forms the shop head, and the mandrel stub drops back into the collection cup.
6. Inspect: the head should sit flat, the parts pulled tight with no gap. A spinning or proud rivet gets drilled out (use the same drill size, cut the head off) and replaced.

## The Long Nose Extension — 48-95-0100

The 6" nose extension threads on between the tool and the nose piece, moving the nose piece half a foot forward. Use it when the rivet sits somewhere the tool body can't:

- Inside **2×1 tube and deep channels**, setting rivets against the far wall.
- Down between mechanisms where the tool's bulk won't fit square to the surface.

Keep the extension square to the work just like the bare tool — the extra length makes tilt easier to introduce and harder to see. It stores in the same Rivet Tools drawer.

## Jaw Cleaning

The pulling jaws inside the nose housing grip the mandrel on every cycle, and they load up with mandrel shavings and coating debris.

**Symptoms of dirty jaws:** the mandrel slips instead of pulling, rivets need multiple trigger pulls, or pull force is noticeably down. **That's your cue — clean the jaws, don't force it.**

1. Battery out.
2. Unscrew the nose housing and remove the jaw assembly (the manual below has the diagram).
3. Brush the jaws clean with a wire brush or solvent — the tooth grooves must be sharp and clear. Do **not** grease the jaw teeth; they grip by friction.
4. Reassemble snug, test-pull a scrap rivet.

If cleaning doesn't restore the pull, the jaws are worn — flag it for a mentor and order a replacement jaw set rather than limping it.

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Empty the mandrel cup into metal recycling.
- Battery out; tool, extension, and nose pieces back in the Rivet Tools drawer.
- Sweep up any dropped mandrels and rivet bodies — floor check before you leave.
- Pull force feels down? Clean the jaws now (see above) so the next user doesn't inherit it.
