The Precision Matthews PM-1130V — the **Blue Lathe** — is our manual lathe for shafts, spacers, hubs, and standoffs: anything round, anything hex-bored, anything that needs a bearing seat. Next to it on the machine-shop bench sits the **Grizzly mill/drill** (the one labeled "Mill"), its companion for flats, keyway slots, and precisely located holes. These two machines carry the strictest rules in the lab, because they are the ones that grab.

## Safety — The Non-Negotiables

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="The chuck key NEVER stays in the chuck">
Your hand goes on the chuck key, the key goes in the chuck, you tighten, and the key comes out — one motion, every time. A chuck key left in a chuck becomes a projectile the instant the spindle starts. If you walk away and the key is in the chuck, you lose lathe privileges. No exceptions, no "just for a second."
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="No gloves. Tie your hair. Nothing loose.">
Rotating machinery grabs and does not let go. **No gloves, ever**, at the lathe or mill — a caught glove pulls your hand in. Long hair tied back and tucked, no loose sleeves, no drawstrings, no lanyards, no jewelry, no rings. This is the strictest application of the [lab glove policy](/safety/gloves-and-ppe/).
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Never leave a running machine">
If the spindle is turning, you are standing at the machine with your hand near the stop. Need to grab a tool, answer a question, check your phone? Spindle off first.
</SafetyWarning>

- Clear chips with a **brush or chip hook after the spindle stops** — never fingers, never a rag near rotation, and long stringy chips get broken up, not pulled.
- Stock sticking out of the back of the spindle bore more than a couple of diameters must be supported or cut shorter — unsupported bar whips and bends violently.
- Know where the E-stop is before you start.
- Measure only with the spindle **stopped**.

## Spindle Speeds

Starting point: **RPM ≈ (4 × SFM) ÷ diameter in inches**. With our carbide insert tooling, use roughly 400–500 SFM for aluminum and ~300 SFM for mild steel; HSS runs about half that.

| Workpiece diameter | Aluminum (carbide) | Mild steel (carbide) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 in | 2000 RPM (max) | 2000 RPM |
| 1 in | 1800–2000 RPM | 1200 RPM |
| 1.5 in | 1200 RPM | 800 RPM |
| 2 in | 900 RPM | 600 RPM |

Adjustments that matter: **parting and knurling run at about 1/3 of turning speed**; drilling from the tailstock uses the drill diameter (not the stock diameter) in the formula; interrupted cuts (hex stock!) run slower. Use the tach, not guesswork, and remember the two belt ranges — low range for big diameters and heavy cuts, high range for small-diameter finishing.

## Core Operations

### Facing

Square the end of the stock first — every other measurement references it. Tool exactly on center height (a tool above or below center leaves a nub), feed from outside to center with the cross slide, light final pass for finish.

### Turning

Rough in 0.020–0.050 in radius passes for aluminum (half that in steel), sneak up on final diameter with a 0.005 in finish pass. Measure with a micrometer from [Orange Toolbox drawer 10](/storage/toolboxes/#orange-toolbox--metrology-machinist-tooling--ppe), and remember the cross slide dial may read radius, not diameter — verify before trusting it.

### Parting

The operation most likely to bite. Part close to the chuck, tool square and exactly on center, spindle in low range at ~1/3 turning speed, steady feed, cutting oil the whole way. If it chatters or squeals, stop and get a mentor — don't push through. Catch the part in a tray, never in your hand.

### Drilling from the Tailstock

Center-face the stock, start with a **center drill**, then step up through drill sizes to your final diameter. Peck: drill about one diameter deep, back out, clear chips, repeat. Oil in steel. Spare drill chucks and Morse-taper arbors are in **Orange Toolbox drawer 7**; the MT2 live center and the boring/turning tool holders are in **drawer 5**.

### Tapping

Power tapping on the lathe is not a student operation. Spindle **off**: start the tap straight using the tailstock chuck (or a spring center) to guide it while you turn the tap by hand with a wrench. For production tapping of many parts, the [electric tapping machine](/cutting/tapping-machine/) on the bench by the chop saws is faster and safer.

## Tooling — the Lathe Drawers

The lathe runs a **quick-change tool post (QCTP)**: each cutter lives in its own holder, pre-set for height, and swaps in seconds. Set tool height to the spindle center (face a point on scrap or use the tailstock center as reference), lock the holder, and put holders back in the drawer with their inserts intact — report chipped inserts, don't hide them.

## The Grizzly Mill/Drill

<LabGalleryImage
  src="grizzly-mill-and-blue-lathe.jpg"
  alt="Grizzly mill/drill with milling vise next to the blue PM-1130V lathe"
  caption="The Grizzly mill/drill (labeled Mill) and the Blue Lathe share the machine-shop bench — and the same safety rules."
/>

The mill/drill handles what the lathe can't: milled flats on hex shaft, slots, pockets, and hole patterns that need real position accuracy. Ground rules:

- **Every part is clamped** — in the milling vise or to the table. Hand-held work on a mill is how parts become projectiles.
- Same speed logic as the lathe, using the **cutter** diameter: aluminum fast, steel slow, small cutters faster than big ones.
- Tighten the drawbar with the T-handle wrenches on the machine, and remove the wrench immediately — same discipline as the chuck key.
- Climb vs conventional: use conventional milling for manual work unless a mentor sets you up otherwise.
- All lathe safety rules apply verbatim: no gloves, hair tied, never leave it running, brush the chips.

## Operating Instructions

1. Run the pre-flight checklist. Hand-rotate the chuck one full revolution — nothing should touch.
2. Start the spindle at the planned RPM and let it come up to speed before feeding.
3. Take a light first cut to verify the setup, then measure (spindle stopped).
4. Rough to within ~0.010 in of final size, then finish-pass to dimension.
5. Deburr the part edges with a file (spindle stopped) or at the [deburring wheel](/cutting/sanders-grinders/).
6. Spindle off, brush chips, part out of the chuck, key out of the chuck.

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Spindle off, power off at the machine.
- Brush all chips into the tray — ways, cross slide, and chuck. Never blow chips with compressed air (it drives grit into the ways).
- Wipe the ways and put a light film of way oil on them; oil points per the manual are on the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).
- Tool holders, measuring tools, and the chuck key back in the lathe drawers.
- Aluminum offcuts go in the recycling bin; steel in the scrap bin.
