The arbor press is the lab's controlled-force tool: a long lever, a rack-and-pinion ram, and your full attention. We use it for two jobs — **pressing bearings** into printed and machined parts, and **broaching** keyways and hex profiles through bored holes. Everything it does could be faked with a hammer, and everything it does is ruined by one.

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Never hammer. Never extend the lever.">
If a part won't press with steady one-arm force on the stock handle, something is misaligned, undersized, or wrong — hammering on the ram or slipping a pipe over the lever turns "stuck" into "shattered." Broaches are hardened and brittle: they don't bend, they explode. Stop and get a mentor.
</SafetyWarning>

- Keep fingers out from under the ram and never between the part and the table. The press doesn't know the difference between a bearing and a fingertip.
- Support the part flat and square on the table plates; a tilted part shoots sideways under load.
- Eye protection on — a snapping broach or spring-loaded part throws hardened fragments.
- Pressing bearings, no gloves needed; see [PPE & the glove policy](/safety/gloves-and-ppe/).

## Pressing Bearings

1. **Check the fit first.** The bearing should start into the bore by hand for the first bit. If you can't start it at all, the bore is undersized — fix the part (re-bore, or reprint with adjusted tolerance), don't force it.
2. **Press only on the race that's being fitted.** Bearing into a housing: press the **outer race**. Bearing onto a shaft: press the **inner race**. Pressing through the balls brinells the races and kills the bearing quietly — it'll feel gritty in a month.
3. Use a flat pressing plate, a socket, or a machined spacer that contacts the correct race evenly all the way around. Never press on the seal or shield.
4. Align the bearing square to the bore, take up the slack gently, and check it's starting straight before applying force.
5. Press with one smooth, steady stroke until it seats. It should feel firm and even — spikes in force mean it's cocked. Back it out and restart rather than powering through.
6. **Printed parts:** plastic bores flex and relax. Design bores for the press fit your filament actually holds (see [print settings](/3d-printing/print-settings/)), press slowly, and let heat-sensitive plastics (PLA especially) cool between attempts. If the boss cracks, the wall was too thin — redesign, don't glue.

## Broaching Keyways & Hex Bores

The keyway broach set — broaches in a wooden box, plus bushings and shims in tubes — lives in the machine-shop drawer along with hand reamers.

<LabGalleryImage
  src="broach-set-drawer.jpg"
  alt="Drawer with keyway broach set in a wooden box, bushings in tubes, and hand reamers"
  caption="The broach set drawer: keyway broaches, guide bushings, and hand reamers. Count the shims back in when you're done."
/>

1. **Bore first, broach second.** Drill/bore or ream the hole to the exact size the broach chart calls for — usually done on the [lathe](/forming/lathes/) or [drill press](/cutting/drill-press-shear/).
2. Pick the **matching guide bushing** for the hole diameter and broach size. The bushing keeps the broach square — never freehand a broach through a bare hole.
3. Rotate the press table so a clearance slot is under the ram — the broach and chips must have somewhere to exit.
4. Oil the broach with cutting oil (both faces), insert it through the bushing, and check it sits square before loading.
5. Press through with one **smooth, continuous stroke** — steady force, no jerking, no stopping mid-cut if you can avoid it. The ram should stay square over the broach the whole stroke.
6. For deeper keyways, add the supplied **shim** behind the broach and take a second pass. Passes are shallow by design; skipping a shim step is how broaches snap.
7. Clear chips from the broach teeth with a brush, re-oil between passes, and wipe the broach clean before it goes back in its slot in the box.

**Hex broaching** works the same way with hex broaches and bushings: round bore to the chart size, bushing, oil, steady stroke. For FRC hex bores in aluminum hubs, chamfer the entry edge first — it helps the broach start square.

## Alignment — the Whole Game

- Ram, tool, part, and table opening in one straight line. Eyeball it from two sides before loading force.
- Take up slack slowly and *feel* the first bit of engagement. Anything crooked reveals itself in the first eighth inch — that's when it's still fixable.
- Force rising smoothly = cutting/pressing. Force spiking = binding. Back off, realign.

## Cleanup

- Brush chips off the table, sweep the slot openings, and re-seat the table plates.
- Wipe broaches and bushings, oil lightly, and return every piece to the wooden box — a missing shim strands the whole set.
- Return pressing adapters and sockets to their drawers.
