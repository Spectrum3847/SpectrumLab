Every other page on this site answers "how do I run this machine." This one answers the question that comes first: **I have a design in <Term id="onshape" />. How do I get a part?**

Four questions decide almost everything:

1. **What material?**
2. **How thick?**
3. **Is it flat, bent, round, or fully 3D?**
4. **What actually has to be accurate?** — usually one bore or one hole pattern, not the whole part.

Answer those and the machine picks itself.

## Which machine makes this part?

| If the part is… | Make it on | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat sheet metal, up to ~1/4 in | [xTool MetalFab](/lasers/xtool-metalfab/) or [Gweike M3](/lasers/gweike-m3-ultra/) | Fastest path for aluminum and steel plate. Leaves a small <Term id="haz" />. |
| Flat metal, thick, hardened, or heat-sensitive | [WAZER Pro](/waterjet/wazer-pro/) | No heat at all, so no <Term id="haz" /> — but slow, and edges have <Term id="taper" />. |
| Flat wood, acrylic, cardboard, leather, <Term id="polycarbonate" />, polypropylene, MaxComposite | [Thunder Nova 35](/lasers/thunder-nova-35/) | CO2. **Never PVC** in any laser. |
| Flat, then bent into a bracket | Laser or waterjet → [Titan 25T](/forming/langmuir-titan-25t/) | Cut the <Term id="flat-pattern" />, not the folded outline. |
| Round: shafts, spacers, bosses, bores | [Lathe](/forming/lathes/) | The only machine here that makes a truly concentric bore. |
| Holes that must land on exact coordinates; tube ends squared | [Mill/Drill](/forming/mill/) | Position from an edge on the X/Y table instead of aiming at a punch mark. |
| Tube and extrusion cut to length | [Evolution chop saw](/cutting/evolution-saws/) or [bandsaw](/cutting/bandsaws/) | Use a <Term id="stop-block" /> for repeat cuts. |
| A hole pattern in existing stock | [Drill press](/cutting/drill-press-shear/) + [tapping machine](/cutting/tapping-machine/) | Print the drawing 1:1 and use it as a layout template. |
| Complex 3D geometry, low load | [Prusa farm](/3d-printing/prusa-farm/) | <Term id="petg-gf" /> for anything on the robot. |
| Complex 3D geometry, high load or heat | [Bambu fleet](/3d-printing/bambu-fleet/) | <Term id="ppa-gf" /> and multi-material jobs. |
| Vinyl, stickers, shirt graphics | [Cricut](/graphics/cricut-maker-4/) / [heat press](/graphics/heat-press/) | <Term id="kiss-cut" /> for stickers, <Term id="htv" /> for apparel. |
| A full-size paper template | [HP T630 plotter](/graphics/hp-t630-plotter/) | Print at 1:1 and tape it to the stock. |

When two machines could do it, the tiebreakers are: **is a machine free right now**, **which one is somebody signed off on**, and **how much post-processing each leaves you**.

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="PVC never goes in a laser">
**PVC** is banned from every laser in this lab — it releases chlorine gas that damages your lungs and corrodes the machine from the inside out. This applies no matter how thin the sheet is or how quick the job. Polycarbonate is fine on the CO2 lasers; it just needs moderate power/speed and good air assist, same as any other plastic here.
</SafetyWarning>

## Fix these in CAD, before you export

Most parts that come out wrong were wrong in the model. Four things to check:

### 1. Holes are one of two sizes, and they are not the same size

Every hole is either a <Term id="clearance-fit">clearance hole</Term> (the bolt passes through) or a <Term id="tap-drill">tap drill</Term> (threads get cut into it). Getting these backwards is the single most common mistake on this site's most common fasteners:

| Fastener | Clearance hole | Tap drill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Term id="ten-thirty-two">#10-32</Term> | 0.201 in (#7) | 0.159 in (#21) |
| <Term id="quarter-twenty">1/4-20</Term> | 0.266 in (17/64) | 0.201 in (#7) |

Read that twice: **0.201 in is the clearance hole for #10-32 and the tap drill for 1/4-20.** Model the number, not the fraction, and label it in the drawing.

### 2. Corners shouldn't be sharp

Anything cut by a rotating tool has a corner radius, so a part designed with a small internal radius is more forgiving to make on any machine and much less likely to crack in service. External corners need fillets too — a sharp outside corner is a cut hazard and a stress riser.

### 3. If it bends, use sheet metal features

Model bent parts with Onshape's sheet metal tools, not as a folded solid. That gets you a <Term id="flat-pattern" /> with correct <Term id="bend-allowance" />, automatic <Term id="bend-relief" />, and a bend table. Set the <Term id="k-factor" /> to match the material and thickness — and confirm it with a test bend before you cut ten of them.

Also: keep holes away from bend lines. A hole too close to a bend distorts into an oval.

### 4. Pick the alloy on purpose

<Term id="five-oh-five-two">5052</Term> is the alloy for anything that gets bent. For flat parts, <Term id="five-oh-five-two">5052</Term>, <Term id="six-oh-six-one">6061</Term>, and <Term id="seven-oh-seven-five">7075</Term> are all fine — strength and stiffness go up in that order. It's okay to use 5052 for flat parts, and we do a good amount. Bending 6061 to a tight radius cracks it — that is a design decision, not a brake problem.

## Exporting from Onshape

The format depends on what the receiving machine reads, not on what you have open.

| Going to | Export | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lasers, waterjet, Cricut | <Term id="dxf" /> | Right-click the flat face → **Export as DXF/DWG**. For sheet metal, export the **flat pattern**, not the model. |
| 3D printers | <Term id="three-mf">3MF</Term>, <Term id="stl" />, or <Term id="step" /> | Right-click the part → **Export**. 3MF if you want your settings to travel with it. STEP works too — our slicers read it directly. |
| Anyone who needs to modify or machine it | <Term id="step" /> | Right-click the part → **Export → STEP**. |
| A person at a machine | PDF drawing | Make a Drawing tab, dimension what matters, export PDF. |
| Engraving art, logos | <Term id="svg" /> | From the graphics tool, not from CAD. |

Two things that go wrong at export:

- **Exporting the folded part instead of the flat pattern.** The outline of a bent bracket is not a shape you can cut.
- **Exporting a stale version.** An export is a snapshot. Change the model afterward and your DXF is quietly wrong. Re-export after every change, and put the date in the filename.

## Handing it to the machine

| Machine | Software | It wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| xTool MetalFab | XCS / MetalFab software | DXF, plus material and thickness selected in the machine profile |
| Gweike M3 Ultra | MLaser | DXF or SVG; correct mode selected before anything else |
| Thunder Nova 35 | LightBurn | DXF or SVG, with layers assigned to <Term id="vector">cut</Term>, <Term id="score" />, and <Term id="raster" /> |
| WAZER Pro | WAM | DXF; material and thickness drive the cut-quality setting |
| Titan 25T | BendControl | Bend sequence and <Term id="backgauge" /> positions, from your bend table |
| Bambu fleet | Bambu Studio | 3MF or STL → sliced to <Term id="gcode" /> |
| Prusa farm | PrusaSlicer | 3MF or STL → sliced to G-code |
| Cricut | Design Space | SVG or DXF |

In every case the machine's own software does the <Term id="cam" /> step — assigning <Term id="toolpath">toolpaths</Term>, <Term id="lead-in">lead-ins</Term>, and <Term id="nesting" />. That's where you set them, not in Onshape.

## After the cut

A part is rarely done when it comes off the machine. The usual order, and it is an order for a reason:

1. **Cut** — laser, waterjet, or saw.
2. **<Term id="deburr" />** — every edge, both sides. Do this before anything else touches the part; burrs stop parts sitting flat in the brake and ruin coating adhesion.
3. **Drill and <Term id="tap" /> any holes that weren't cut in.** Tapping is easier on a flat blank than on a bent part.
4. **Bend** on the Titan. Bending after tapping is fine; bending after coating is not.
5. **Test fit the assembly.** This is the last cheap moment to find a mistake.
6. **Finish** — [surface prep](/finishing/surface-prep/), then [powder coat](/finishing/powder-coating/) and [cure](/finishing/curing-oven/). <Term id="masking">Mask</Term> threads and bearing bores first.

The two sequencing mistakes that cost the most time: **powder coating before test fitting** (now the coat has to come off), and **bending before deburring** (now the burr is folded inside a corner you can't reach).

## Common mistakes

- **Designing to what the machine can do rather than what the part needs.** Tight tolerances everywhere means slow, expensive parts. Put the tolerance on the one bore that carries a bearing.
- **Forgetting the part gets thicker.** Powder coat adds real thickness — a coated part will not slide into a slot cut for a bare one.
- **Sending an STL to be machined.** An <Term id="stl" /> is triangles with no units and no features. Send a <Term id="step" />.
- **Cutting ten before proving one.** The first one off the machine is a test piece. Measure it.
- **No datum.** Dimensioning every feature from the last one stacks all the error into the last hole. Pick a <Term id="datum" /> and measure everything from it.
