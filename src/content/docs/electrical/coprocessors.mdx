The roboRIO is busy running the robot, so vision runs somewhere else: a small single-board computer (SBC) on the robot processes camera frames — primarily **AprilTag detection with PhotonVision** for pose estimation and targeting — and sends results to the roboRIO over the network. This page is the practical checklist for setting one up; the deep documentation lives at WPILib and PhotonVision (links below).

Vision hardware — boards, cameras, cables, and spare SD cards — lives in the **Vision bin on the Green Shelf** in the left closet.

## What We Use Them For

- **AprilTag localization:** cameras + PhotonVision give the robot field-relative pose for auto-alignment and odometry correction.
- **Target/game-piece detection** when the season calls for it.
- **Driver cameras** are the *low*-effort case — a coprocessor is overkill for a plain video feed, but often carries one anyway since it's there.

## Imaging an SD Card

Coprocessors boot from an SD card, and SD cards are the most common failure point. Treat them as disposable and keep spares imaged.

1. Download the current **PhotonVision image for your specific board** from the PhotonVision docs/releases — images are board-specific.
2. Flash it with Raspberry Pi Imager or balenaEtcher. Verify after write if the tool offers it.
3. Use a **name-brand, high-endurance card**, 16GB+. Mystery cards corrupt at the worst time.
4. **Label the card** (board + date) with the label maker, and image a spare while you're at it. A pre-imaged spare card in the pit turns a dead coprocessor into a two-minute fix.
5. Power the board off cleanly before pulling a card — yanking a card under power corrupts it.

## Network Setup

Robot networking follows the WPILib convention — for team 3847 the robot network is `10.38.47.x`:

- Radio: `10.38.47.1` · roboRIO: `10.38.47.2`
- **Give each coprocessor a static IP** starting at `10.38.47.11` (`.12`, `.13` for additional units), subnet `255.255.255.0`. Set it in the PhotonVision web UI's networking settings.
- Static beats mDNS (`photonvision.local`) at competition — mDNS discovery is flaky on the field; a static IP means the roboRIO and your dashboard always find the camera.
- Plug the coprocessor into the radio's extra ethernet port or the robot's network switch. Configure the radio itself per the WPILib radio programming docs each season.
- Bookmark the PhotonVision UI (`http://10.38.47.11:5800`) on the drive laptop.

## Mounting & Power

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Never power a coprocessor from the roboRIO USB port">
The roboRIO's USB ports cannot supply the current an SBC pulls under vision load. The board will brown out mid-match — usually exactly when the code asks it for a pose. Power coprocessors from a dedicated regulated supply off the power distribution board.
</SafetyWarning>

- **Power:** use a **buck converter / regulated supply** rated comfortably above the board's peak draw (vision boards spike hard under load — budget 3A+ at 5V for small boards, and check your board's requirement). Feed it from a protected channel on the power distribution board or a dedicated regulator module. Solid crimped connections per the [wire prep page](/electrical/wire-prep-crimping/) — a vibrating power lead is a rebooting coprocessor.
- **Bench-test first:** bring the board up on the [current-limited bench supply](/electrical/soldering-test-bench/) and watch its actual current draw before robot install.
- **Mounting:** hard-mount with standoffs or a printed case, with airflow to the SoC. Add vibration isolation (rubber grommets, TPU mounts) — SD cards and vibration are enemies.
- **Protect the SD card** physically; a card sticking out of a board edge on a robot will get sheared.
- **Strain-relieve everything:** camera USB/CSI cables and power leads zip-tied so connectors carry no load. Locking USB or taped connectors for cameras.
- Mount cameras rigidly — calibration is only as good as the camera's pose. Re-run PhotonVision calibration after any camera remount.
