The Light Armor curing oven is the second half of [powder coating](/finishing/powder-coating/): it melts the electrostatically-clung powder into a smooth, hard finish. It's a hand-built dedicated powder-curing oven — PID temperature controller with a timer, ceramic door seal, and hanging rails inside. It is not a kitchen appliance and nothing edible ever goes in it.

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Everything about this oven is hot">
The racks, the hooks, the parts, and the door hardware are all at ~400°F during a cure. **Heat-resistant gloves for any reach into the oven — no exceptions.** A 400°F part looks exactly like a room-temperature part. Assume everything inside is hot until it has provably cooled.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Never leave a running oven unattended">
Someone stays in the building, aware the oven is on, for the entire cure. Set the timer *and* a phone timer. The last-mentor-out closing check includes verifying the oven is **off and cool** — don't be the reason that check exists.
</SafetyWarning>

- Keep the area in front of the door clear — you need room to load and unload without touching anything.
- No flammables near the oven: no solvent rags, no powder bottles, no cardboard stacked against it.
- Parts must be fully dry before curing. Solvent trapped in a masked bore flashes to vapor at temperature.
- Watch your forearms on the door frame when reaching deep — that's the classic burn.

## Reading a Cure Schedule

Powder doesn't cure on a universal timer — each powder has a <Term id="cure-schedule">cure schedule</Term> on its data sheet. Our Prismatic Powders colors each have a per-color PDF (find it on the [Prismatic documents page](https://www.prismaticpowders.com/documents)) that says something like "10 minutes at 400°F **part metal temperature**." Two things students get wrong:

1. **The clock starts when the *metal* reaches temperature, not when you close the door.** A 1/16" bracket gets to 400°F in a couple of minutes; a chunk of 1" plate or a gearbox housing can take 15+ minutes just to come up to temp. Thicker part = longer total oven time.
2. **Full cure window is typically 10–20 minutes at part temperature for our powders.** Undercured powder looks fine but chips like a candy shell; overcuring (within reason) mostly just yellows whites — when unsure, err slightly long.

Rule of thumb: watch through the first few minutes — when the powder flows out from matte dust to a wet-look gloss (~400°F for our powders), the surface is at temp. Start the schedule clock a few minutes after flow-out for thin parts, longer for thick ones.

## Operating Instructions

1. **Preheat.** Set the PID to the powder's cure temperature (typically ~400°F) and let the oven reach and hold setpoint before loading. Loading a cold oven gives you an unknown cure clock.
2. **Load without touching anything.** Uncured powder is held on by static only. Carry parts by their hooks from the booth and hang them on the oven rails. If a part brushes the door frame on the way in, it goes back to the booth for a touch-up.
3. **Space the parts out.** Parts must not touch each other or the walls — every contact point becomes a bare, ugly spot, and touching parts fuse together.
4. **Set the timer.** Use the powder's schedule *at part temperature* (see above). Thin brackets: schedule time plus a few minutes. Thick or cast parts: add soak time for the metal to come up to temp.
5. **Monitor.** Stay in the area. Check that the PID is holding setpoint and not overshooting. If you smell strong burning (faint powder smell is normal), kill the oven and get a mentor.
6. **End of cure: oven off, door cracked or parts out with heat gloves.** Move parts (by their hooks) to a safe cooling spot where nobody will grab them.
7. **Cool completely before handling.** Parts come out at ~400°F and stay dangerous for a long time. Let them air-cool until you can hold your hand near the surface — don't quench, don't rush. The finish is also soft until it cools.

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Label the cooling zone">
A freshly-cured purple bracket sitting on the bench is a trap for the next person who walks by. Put a "HOT PARTS" note next to cooling parts, or cool them inside the powder room where traffic is low.
</SafetyWarning>

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Oven **off at the controller and the switch** when the last cure ends. The daily closing checklist has the last mentor out confirming the oven is off and cool.
- Pull any dropped hooks or powder flakes off the oven floor once cool.
- Keep the door seal clean — don't slam the door on hanging wires.
- **Monthly** (per [Maintenance Schedules](/maintenance/schedules/)): verify the PID reading against a reference thermometer, and inspect the ceramic door seal. If the PID and reference disagree by more than a few degrees, tag the oven and tell a mentor — every cure depends on that number being true.
