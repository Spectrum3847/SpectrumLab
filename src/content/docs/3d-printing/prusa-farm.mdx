The Prusa Mini+ farm — twelve printers racked in wooden cubbies under the printer bench — is
the volume workhorse of the lab. It handles most of the 3D-printed part demand for both the
main team and the junior team: PLA prototypes, PETG-GF brackets, jigs, and the endless stream of
"can you print me a..." requests. The Minis are simple, tough, and the best place for newer
students to learn printing without tying up the [Bambu fleet](/3d-printing/bambu-fleet/).

A few of the twelve are earmarked to be **donated to other FRC teams** — check the farm
whiteboard before you plan a twelve-printer batch, since the working count changes.

## Farm standards

The farm is tuned for **throughput**, not fine detail — that is the whole point of running twelve
of them.

- **Nozzles: 0.6 mm hardened steel** across the farm. Hardened so PETG-GF is safe to run
  anywhere; 0.6 mm so parts come off in a fraction of the time.
- **Line width 0.70–0.75 mm** (115–125% of nozzle) and **0.28–0.32 mm layers** — wide, thick
  beads that build strength fast. See the
  [slicer priority hierarchy](/3d-printing/print-settings/#the-slicer-priority-hierarchy).
- **Walls: 4–5** on a 0.6 mm nozzle for a structural part, not the 6–8 you would run on a Bambu.
- Sheets are **textured PEI**, matching the Bambu fleet standard.

## Safety

- The nozzle runs at up to 280 °C and the bed at up to 100 °C. Keep fingers clear during
  printing and heating.
- The Minis are open-frame — no enclosure means no barrier. Do not lean tools, spools, or
  sleeves into a running printer's motion path.
- Materials: **PETG-GF and PLA** on the farm. Flexibles fight the Bowden extruder, so
  TPU goes to a Bambu — hard 72D runs through the AMS, flow-capped.
- **No PPA-GF on the Minis.** The hotend tops out at 280 °C, well under PPA-GF's 310 °C, and
  an open frame cannot hold a heated chamber. Take it to the
  [H2D](/3d-printing/bambu-fleet/) — it's the only machine that runs it. CF nylon and PC from
  existing stock go on any enclosed machine, X2D included.


## Software & Workflow — PrusaSlicer

1. Open **PrusaSlicer** and select the **Original Prusa MINI & MINI+** printer profile, then set
   the nozzle to **0.6 mm** — the default profile assumes 0.4 mm and will under-extrude badly.
2. Pick a print profile: **0.30 mm SPEED** for most work, **0.20 mm QUALITY** when surface
   finish matters.
3. Pick the matching filament profile. PLA and PETG have well-tuned stock profiles; **PETG-GF
   has no stock profile — start from Generic PETG** and raise the nozzle to the top of the
   PETG-GF range. Do not hand-edit temperatures beyond that unless a mentor asks you to.
4. Orient, support, and set walls/infill per
   [Print Settings & Supports](/3d-printing/print-settings/).
5. Slice and check the preview — time, filament, supports.
6. Send the job through **Prusa Connect** (preferred, see below) or export G-code to a USB
   stick and start it at the printer.

### Prusa Connect queue

The Minis are on the lab network and registered to the team's Prusa Connect account:

1. Sign in to Prusa Connect on the shop PC.
2. Upload your sliced file and add it to the **queue** of any idle printer — the dashboard
   shows every Mini's state, temperature, and progress at a glance.
3. During build season, do not cherry-pick "your" printer: take the first idle machine so the
   queue drains evenly.
4. Check the dashboard before physically visiting the farm — it saves you a trip when
   everything is busy.

Two things make that dashboard worth trusting:

- **Every Mini has a camera.** The farm runs ESP32-CAM boards flashed as Prusa Connect webcams
  (the team maintains its own build of the firmware), so the dashboard shows a live still of each
  bed, not just a progress bar. Look at the picture before you assume a job is fine — spaghetti
  reads as "printing" to the printer.
- **The Minis run input-shaping firmware**, added along with the Wi-Fi modules and cameras
  ([Lab Upgrades, Jan 2024](https://www.spectrum3847.org/seasons/2024/#2024-01-05-lab-upgrades)). That is why farm print times are closer to
  the Bambus than a stock Mini's would be — and why a profile copied off the internet with stock
  Mini accelerations is leaving time on the table.

## Operating Instructions

1. **Load filament:** Preheat for your material from the LCD menu, snip the filament end at
   45°, feed it into the extruder inlet, and confirm the load when the LCD asks. Watch for a
   clean colored line of purge.
2. **Start the print** from the Prusa Connect queue or the USB stick via the LCD knob.
3. **Watch the first layer.** The Mini auto-levels with mesh bed leveling, but Live-Z decides
   whether the first layer sticks. If it is not going down clean, stop and fix — see below.
4. When done, pull the sheet off, **flex it over the bin** to pop the part free, and put the
   sheet straight back on the pins. Sheets that wander the lab get lost and bent.

### Live-Z calibration

Live-Z is the nozzle-to-sheet gap. It is the single most important number on a Mini, and it is
**per printer, per sheet** — check it after every sheet swap (that is on the
[maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/)).

1. From the LCD: **Calibration → First Layer Calibration.** The printer prints a zigzag test
   line and a filled square.
2. While it prints, turn the knob to adjust Live-Z in real time:
   - **Too high:** lines look like round spaghetti sitting on the sheet, with gaps between
     passes. Go lower (more negative).
   - **Too low:** lines are translucent, edges plow up ridges, the nozzle scratches. Go higher.
   - **Right:** lines are slightly flattened ovals fused into their neighbors with no gaps —
     the filled square peels off as one solid patch.
3. Save and print. If a mentor re-set Live-Z, do not tweak it because your part lifted once —
  clean the sheet first; 90% of adhesion problems are grease, not Z.

### Sheet care

- **Fingerprints kill adhesion.** Handle sheets by the edges, always.
- **Every few prints:** wash the sheet with dish soap and warm water, rubbing with a clean
  paper towel; rinse thoroughly and dry with a fresh towel. Soap and water only — no IPA. IPA
  redistributes finger oils rather than removing them and can leave its own residue behind; a
  proper wash is what actually resets adhesion.
- **PETG and PETG-GF weld themselves to smooth PEI** — the farm runs textured sheets for exactly
  this reason. If you must use smooth, glue stick as a release layer.
- Bent, deeply gouged, or bald sheets: flag them to a mentor for replacement, do not quietly
  put them back.

## Listen to the printer

Spectrum's own [3D Printing Training](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/17f3FW7n_B4S9B0Uf2dVvO5ZlcCNTxSpik0KHe84lzBc/preview) deck makes this the first troubleshooting step, and it is genuinely the fastest diagnostic in the farm:

- **Clicking from the extruder** — the drive gear is skipping. The hotend is jammed or the filament isn't moving. Stop the print; don't let it grind a flat spot into the filament.
- **Loud or erratic axis noise** — something on that axis is binding or out of alignment, or the leadscrew wants lubrication. A healthy stepper makes a consistent tone.
- **Fan noise that changes** — a bearing going out. A part-cooling fan that dies mid-print ruins overhangs; a hotend fan that dies causes a jam. Flag it.
- **Silence where there should be a fan** — stop the print and tell a mentor.

Two more from the same deck, worth knowing before you fight a first layer:

- **Moisture matters.** Most of our plastics are hygroscopic. A wet spool means you're boiling water as well as melting plastic: bubbles, popping, weak layers. Dry the spool, don't fight the profile — see the [Filament Guide](/3d-printing/filament-guide/).
- **Glue stick is a release layer as often as an adhesive.** Some filaments stick *too* well to a sheet and take the coating with them; a glue layer lets go before the sheet does.

## When to use a Mini vs a Bambu

| Situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| PLA prototype, jig, or fixture | **Mini** — always |
| PETG-GF part, no rush, fits 18 cm cube | **Mini** — the farm exists for this |
| Batch of 8 identical parts | **Minis** — one per printer beats one big plate |
| Junior team parts | **Minis** — their home turf |
| Deadline tonight, needs to print fast | **Bambu** |
| Fine detail or a thin-walled part | **H2D / X2D** — the only 0.4 mm machines in the lab. The X1Cs are 0.6 mm like the Minis, so they gain you nothing here |
| PPA-GF, CF nylon, PC, or TPU | **Bambu only** |
| Part bigger than 18 cm | [Bambu](/3d-printing/bambu-fleet/) — up to 350 × 320 × 325 mm on the H2D |
| Zero-gap or soluble supports | **H2D / X2D** (dual nozzle) |

## Cleanup & Maintenance

After your print:

- Part off, sheet flexed clean and back on the pins, scraps in the trash.
- Spool back to storage if you unloaded; tape the filament end.
- Update/clear your job in Prusa Connect so the printer shows idle.

On the schedule (see [Maintenance Schedules](/maintenance/schedules/)):

- **Weekly:** wash PEI sheets with dish soap and warm water during the farm walk-down.
- **As needed:** an extra soap-and-water wash any time adhesion drops before the next scheduled one.
- **Per sheet change:** verify Live-Z with a first-layer calibration.
