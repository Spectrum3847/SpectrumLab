Material choice matters more than any slicer setting. A perfectly tuned PLA bracket will still
snap at the first hard hit; a mediocre PCTG print of the same part will survive the season.
Pick the material first, then go tune settings on the
[Print Settings & Supports](/3d-printing/print-settings/) page.

Our lineup is chosen for four things, in this order: **FRC field-impact survival**, **print
throughput**, **shop safety in a shared space**, and **mechanical and chemical reliability**
on an assembled robot.

## Which material do I use?

Work down this list and stop at the first line that matches your part:

- **Just checking fit, making a jig, or printing something cosmetic?** → **PLA.** Cheapest,
  fastest, easiest. Not for anything that lives on the robot.
- **Going on the robot?** → **PCTG.** This is the default answer for brackets, gearbox plates,
  covers, and electronics shields. Tough, fast, and chemically stable.
- **Must not flex — motor mount, dead-axle spacer?** → **PETG-GF.** Trade a little toughness
  for a lot of stiffness.
- **Takes direct hits — armor, roller, hard stop, bumper bracket?** → **72D TPU.** Absorbs
  impact instead of cracking, and nothing chemical touches it.
- **Extreme load or lives somewhere hot?** → **PPA-GF.** Gearbox blocks, 185 °C+ HDT mounts.
  H2D or X2D only, dried first, mentor sign-off.

When in doubt: prototype in PLA, ship in PCTG. Escalate to PPA-GF only when PCTG or PETG-GF has
actually failed or the math says it will.

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="PLA melts in a hot car">
PLA softens around 55 °C. Parts left in a car in Houston, near the powder-coat curing oven, or
against a hot motor will sag and warp. If a part sees any heat at all, it is not a PLA part.
</SafetyWarning>

## The approved lineup

All **functional** filaments are stocked in **White or Natural/Clear** — it keeps the team
aesthetic consistent and, more usefully, lets you see internal wall and infill fusion when you
inspect or section a failed part.

| Material | Color / Finish | Density (g/cm³) | Max Flow | Print Speed | Loctite | Primary FRC applications |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **72D TPU** <br/><small>CC3D / Polar / Siraya</small> | Natural (translucent) / solid white | **1.24** | 4–8 mm³/s | 40–80 mm/s | **Immune** | Direct-impact armor, intake rollers, pulleys, hard stops, bumper brackets |
| **PCTG** <br/><small>3D-Fuel / Essentium / Atomic</small> | Optical clear / solid white | **1.24** | 14–20 mm³/s | 150–250+ mm/s | **High** | Primary workhorse: structural brackets, gearbox plates, electronics shields |
| **PETG-GF** <br/><small>Bambu / Polymaker</small> | Matte white | **1.35** | 12–16 mm³/s | 120–200 mm/s | **High** | High-rigidity motor mounts, dead-axle spacers, stiff structural brackets |
| **PPA-GF** <br/><small>Siraya Fibreheart</small> | Bone matte white | **1.32** | 8–12 mm³/s | 50–100 mm/s | **Immune** | Extreme-load gearbox blocks, high-temp mounts (185 °C+ HDT) |

<SafetyWarning type="tip" title="CAD mass properties (Onshape)">
Assign **1.24 g/cm³** for PCTG and 72D TPU, and **1.35 g/cm³** for PETG-GF and PPA-GF. PPA-GF's
true density is 1.32 — we round it up to 1.35 in CAD so glass-filled parts never come out
lighter on paper than on the robot.
</SafetyWarning>

## What we steer away from, and why

None of this is contraband. If it is already on the shelf, **use it up** — just do not design
new parts around it, and do not reorder it.

- **Styrene-based polymers (ABS / ASA / HIPS).** We do not stock these at all. They emit
  styrene VOCs and ultra-fine particles, and the lab is a shared space where that means
  headaches for whoever is working at the next bench. If a slicer profile or a Bambu support
  chart tells you to use ABS or ASA as a support interface, use white PLA instead — see the
  [zero-gap interface trick](/3d-printing/print-settings/#universal-zero-gap-interface).
- **Carbon-fiber nylon (PA-CF / PPA-CF / PET-CF).** Excellent stiffness, wrong failure mode for
  impact mechanisms: the chopped fibers act as micro-stress risers under shock load, so parts
  shatter along layer lines in a collision rather than bending. Spend our existing CF spools on
  **static** parts — sensor mounts, electronics trays, fixturing — and reach for PPA-GF or 72D
  TPU for anything that gets hit.
- **Polycarbonate (PC) and acrylic (PMMA).** These craze — fine crack networks that turn into
  fractures — on contact with anaerobic threadlockers like **Loctite 242/262**, essentially
  immediately. Keep PC off any joint that gets threadlocked. It is still the right pick for
  heat-resistant, non-threadlocked parts: light pipes, guards near warm motors, curing-oven
  fixturing.

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Loctite and polycarbonate do not mix">
Anaerobic threadlocker wicks into PC and starts environmental stress cracking on contact. There
is no cure time to wait out and no cleaning it off after. If a printed part gets Loctite
anywhere near it, print it in PCTG, PETG-GF, 72D TPU, or PPA-GF.
</SafetyWarning>

## The materials

### PCTG — the on-robot workhorse

<FilamentCard id="pctg" />

PCTG is what PETG wanted to be: same easy handling and forgiving first layer, noticeably tougher,
and roughly twice the volumetric flow so a plate of brackets comes off in half the time. It needs
no enclosure and no hardened nozzle, which means every printer in the lab can run it. This is the
default.

### PETG-GF — stiffness where flex is the enemy

<FilamentCard id="petg-gf" />

Glass fill trades impact toughness for rigidity. Use it when deflection is the failure mode — a
motor mount that must hold gear mesh, a dead-axle spacer that must not compress. Do not use it
where the part takes hits; that is PCTG's or 72D TPU's job. Abrasive: hardened nozzle only, and
never a 0.2 mm.

### 72D TPU — impact armor

<FilamentCard id="tpu-72d" />

This is not the squishy TPU you are picturing. At Shore 72D it prints and bolts like a rigid
plastic, but it absorbs a hit and springs back where PLA or CF nylon would shatter. It is also
chemically immune to everything in the shop, threadlockers included. Feed it from an external
spool holder on a direct-drive extruder, and keep volumetric flow capped — see the
[Bambu fleet](/3d-printing/bambu-fleet/) page for the ≤ 6 mm³/s rule.

### PPA-GF — extreme load and high temperature

<FilamentCard id="ppa-gf" />

Glass-filled nylon, 185 °C+ HDT, immune to Loctite, and the strongest thing we print for
sustained load. The costs are real: 300–320 °C nozzle, heated chamber, mandatory drying before
every session, and glass dust that needs an N95 and eye protection during any sanding. H2D or
X2D only, and a mentor signs off before it starts.

### PLA — prototypes, jigs, and support interface

<FilamentCard id="pla" />

PLA has two jobs. First, it is the cheapest way to learn that your CAD is wrong — print the fit
check, find the mistake, reprint the real part in PCTG. Second, it is the **support interface
material in Nozzle 2**, because PLA refuses to chemically weld to polyurethanes or copolyesters.
That refusal is what lets us run a zero-gap support interface and get an injection-molded
underside that snaps clean off.

### Already on the shelf

These are materials we own and will finish, but would not choose for a new design. Full
rationale in [what we steer away from](#what-we-steer-away-from-and-why) above, and the
brand-by-brand list of what is physically on the shelf is in the
[Filament Inventory](/inventory/filaments/).

## Storage & drying

Filament is a sponge. Wet filament pops, strings, prints weak layers, and in the case of nylon
produces parts with a fraction of their rated strength.

**Storage rules:**

- Open spools live in **dry boxes with desiccant** — not loose on the bench. Reseal partial
  spools in a zip bag with a desiccant pack if a dry box is full.
- Recharge or replace desiccant when the indicator changes color. Saturated desiccant is
  decoration, not protection.
- PPA-GF and the CF nylons stay in a dry box (or the AMS HT, which actively dries) at all times.
  They go straight from drying to the printer.

**Who must be dried, and how much:**

| Material | Drying requirement |
| --- | --- |
| PLA | Usually fine from the box. 50 °C for 8 h only if it strings. |
| PCTG | 65 °C for 8 h if stored open more than ~2 weeks or if stringing. |
| PETG / PETG-GF | 65 °C for 8 h if stored open more than ~2 weeks or if stringing. |
| 72D TPU | **Required.** 55 °C for 6–8 h before printing. |
| TPU 95A / TPE | **Required.** 55–65 °C for 6–8 h — wet TPU bubbles and strings badly. |
| PPA-GF | **Mandatory, every session.** 80 °C for 4–6 h. |
| PA-CF / PPA-CF | **Mandatory, every session.** 80 °C for 12 h. |
| PC | 80 °C for 8–12 h; hygroscopic. |

### Using the AMS HT

- Run the **internal active dry cycle** before a print rather than trusting a spool that has
  been sitting. Once the chamber is sealed with fresh desiccant it holds low RH on its own.
- For **long prints in actively heated material**, feed from the **rear bypass port** straight
  to the toolhead instead of running the internal drive feeder — it keeps the filament in the
  heated chamber without cycling it through the feed path for hours.

Use a filament dryer or the AMS HT for drying. Do **not** use the powder-coat curing oven — its
temperature swings will cook a spool into a solid donut.

<SafetyWarning type="tip" title="Symptoms of wet filament">
Popping or hissing at the nozzle, steam wisps, stringing that tuning does not fix, fuzzy matte
surfaces, and weak layer adhesion. If you see two or more of these, stop and dry the spool —
you are wasting printer time otherwise.
</SafetyWarning>

## Buying filament

- **Functional filament is ordered White or Natural/Clear only.** Color goes on cosmetic and
  prototype prints, not on structural parts.
- **Bambu-brand spools come from the Bambu store** (reorder links on each card above). The RFID
  tags auto-configure the AMS and slicer profiles, which removes a whole class of student error.
- **PCTG is the bulk staple** — keep several spools ahead of demand during build season. PETG-GF
  is a steady second. PPA-GF is special-order and a mentor approves the purchase.
- **Finish existing stock before reordering.** Open PETG, TPU 95A, CF nylon, and PC spools get
  used up on the parts described above rather than written off. The
  [Filament Inventory](/inventory/filaments/) lists every spool type we own, so check there
  before ordering anything.
- New spool arrives? Label it with the date opened, then into a dry box or the AMS.
