The Bambu fleet is the fast lane of the printer farm: two X1-Carbons — **Bambu 1** and
**Bambu 2** — plus an **H2D** and an **X2D** (more X2Ds are on the way). AMS coverage differs per
machine, so check before you plan a multi-material job: Bambu 1 has a 4-slot AMS *and* an AMS-HT,
Bambu 2 has an AMS-HT only, the H2D carries an AMS Pro on its right nozzle and an AMS-HT on its
left, and the X2D has no AMS yet (an AMS-HT is on order).
These are the lab's most capable printers, the only enclosed machines, and the
only ones with a second nozzle for dual-material supports. If a part is going on the competition
robot, it probably comes off one of these.

## Fleet standards

These apply to **every** Bambu in the lab. They are not per-job choices.

- **Nozzles: hardened steel, fleet-wide — but the size differs by machine.** The **X1Cs run
  0.6 mm**; the **H2D and X2D run 0.4 mm**. Check which machine you are slicing for before you
  pick a profile, because a 0.4 mm profile sent to a 0.6 mm machine under-extrudes badly.
  Hardened is the standard even on materials that do not strictly need it — it means any spool
  can go in any machine without someone checking first. Carbon- and glass-filled filament runs
  on hardened nozzles only, no exceptions.
- **Plates: double-sided textured PEI spring steel, fleet-wide.** One plate type across the farm
  removes a whole class of slicer mismatch errors.
- **Wash plates periodically with warm water and Dawn dish soap.** IPA alone redistributes
  finger oils rather than removing them; a soap-and-water wash in the sink is what actually
  resets adhesion. Wipe with 90%+ IPA between prints, wash properly when adhesion gets flaky.

Bed temperature by material:

| Material | Bed |
| --- | --- |
| 72D TPU | 45–55 °C |
| PCTG / PETG-GF | 70–80 °C |
| PPA-GF | 85–95 °C |

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Cap TPU volumetric flow at 6 mm³/s">
72D TPU will push faster than the extruder gears can grip it. Above roughly 6 mm³/s the gears
grind a flat into the filament instead of feeding it, which jams the toolhead and costs a hotend
teardown. Set the volumetric speed limit in the filament profile before you slice.
</SafetyWarning>

**Material assignments across the fleet:**

- **PCTG, PETG-GF, PLA, PETG** — any Bambu, any time.
- **72D TPU** — any Bambu, **fed through the AMS**, flow-capped at 6 mm³/s.
- **PPA-GF** — **H2D or X2D only.** It needs 300–320 °C and a heated chamber; the X1C's 300 °C
  hotend cannot hold the top of that range reliably.
- **CF nylon and PC (existing stock)** — enclosed machines only, which means all four Bambus.
  See the [Filament Guide](/3d-printing/filament-guide/) for what these are still good for.

The X1Cs are the fleet's reliable middle: enclosed, lidar first-layer inspection, and 0.6 mm
hardened nozzles that turn parts around fast. They run everything except PPA-GF, which needs more
heat than a 300 °C hotend holds. Their AMS setups are **not** identical — Bambu 1 has a 4-slot AMS
plus an AMS-HT, Bambu 2 has only an AMS-HT — so a four-material job has to go on Bambu 1.

The H2D is the fleet's big gun and our **largest build envelope** — 350 × 320 × 325 mm. Heated
chamber, dual nozzles, and two AMS units: an **AMS Pro on the right nozzle** and an **AMS-HT on the
left**. The AMS-HT actively dries filament, so PPA-GF and the CF nylons live in it and stay
print-ready — which also means the **left** nozzle is the one that feeds them. The dual nozzle is
what makes the
[zero-gap PLA support interface](/3d-printing/print-settings/#universal-zero-gap-interface)
possible.

If a part will not fit the H2D, **split it** and bolt or pin the halves — see
[Print Settings](/3d-printing/print-settings/). We do not have a larger printer.

The X2D is the second machine cleared for **PPA-GF** and high-temp engineering work, which takes
that load off the H2D during build season. **More X2Ds are on order** — as they arrive they
become the default home for engineering-material jobs.

**Watch the envelope when you run dual-nozzle.** Single-nozzle gives you the full
256 × 256 × 260 mm, but switching to the dual or auxiliary nozzle costs you about 20 mm of
width and 4 mm of height — **235.5 × 256 × 256 mm**. A part that just fits in single-nozzle mode
can fail to slice once you add a
[zero-gap PLA support interface](/3d-printing/print-settings/#universal-zero-gap-interface).
Check the plate preview after you assign the second nozzle, not before.

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Hot and fast">
The nozzle runs up to 300–350 °C and the toolhead moves at up to 1000 mm/s. Never reach into a
printer while it is printing, homing, or calibrating. Pause the print and wait for motion to
stop before touching anything inside.
</SafetyWarning>

- The bed runs up to 110 °C on engineering materials. Let plates cool before grabbing them
  bare-handed.
- Keep lids and doors set for the material: lid off / door open for PLA and TPU (heat creep),
  closed for PPA-GF and the enclosed engineering materials.
- IPA is flammable — never spray it on a hot bed. Wipe plates on the bench, not in the machine.
- Do not defeat or ignore a paused print: if the machine paused itself, it found a problem.
  Read the screen before resuming.

## Software & Workflow — Bambu Studio

1. Open **Bambu Studio** on the shop PC and make sure you are on the team login.
2. **Pick the right device.** In the Device tab, select Bambu 1, Bambu 2, the H2D, or the X2D.
   Check its camera view first — make sure the plate is empty and nobody else's job is queued.
3. Import your model, orient and arrange it (see
   [Print Settings & Supports](/3d-printing/print-settings/)).
4. **Confirm the plate type is set to textured PEI.** That is the only plate we run, but a
   profile imported from MakerWorld can arrive set to Cool Plate or Engineering Plate — a
   mismatch ruins the first layer.
5. **Map filaments to AMS slots.** Click the filament chips and sync with the AMS. Bambu spools
   auto-identify via RFID; third-party spools must be assigned manually — set material type
   *and* color so the next person can trust the display.
6. Slice, sanity-check the preview (time, filament use, support placement), then **Send** to
   the selected printer. Enable timelapse if you want it; leave AI failure detection on.
7. Monitor the first layer from the camera view or the Bambu Handy app. Do not walk away until
   layer one is down clean.

## Operating Instructions

### Loading the AMS

1. Cut the filament end at ~45° so it feeds cleanly.
2. Open the AMS lid, feed the end into an empty slot until the feeder grabs it and the slot
   LED confirms.
3. Seat the spool so it unwinds toward the feed tube without rubbing the AMS walls.
4. Bambu spools identify themselves via RFID. For third-party spools, set material and color in
   Bambu Studio or on the touchscreen immediately — an unlabeled slot is a trap for the next
   person.

### Unloading

1. From the touchscreen or Studio, select the slot and choose **Unload**.
2. Wait for the printer to purge and retract fully, then pull the spool and re-bag or dry-box
   it. Tape the filament end to the spool — loose ends cause tangles that kill overnight prints.

### Starting and watching a print

1. Send the job from Bambu Studio (workflow above).
2. The printer auto-levels and, on the X1Cs, **lidar-inspects the first layer**. If it flags a
   problem, stop — do not override. Fix the plate (clean, reseat, correct type) and restart.
3. The **AI camera watches for spaghetti failures** and pauses the print if detached plastic is
   flying around. If you get the notification, inspect via camera before resuming or canceling.
4. When the print finishes, let the plate cool a couple of minutes (parts self-release as the
   plate cools), then flex the plate to pop parts off. Never chisel parts off a plate on the
   bed — you will bend the plate or gouge the PEI.

### Nozzle / hotend swap (mentor sign-off required)

The X1-Carbons swap the **whole hotend assembly**, not just a nozzle tip:

1. Power off and let the hotend cool completely.
2. Pop off the magnetic toolhead front cover and disconnect the hotend fan connector.
3. Loosen the hotend retaining screws, swap in the replacement hotend, reconnect, and refit the
   cover. Follow the Bambu Wiki guide the first time — with a mentor.
4. Confirm nozzle size and type on the touchscreen and in Bambu Studio.
5. **Run full calibration (lidar + vibration) after every nozzle change** before the machine
   returns to service.

The H2D and X2D use guided quick-swap nozzles — follow the on-screen maintenance flow. The same
rule applies on every machine: **the fleet standard is hardened steel — 0.6 mm on the X1Cs, 0.4 mm
on the H2D and X2D — and filled filament only ever runs through hardened nozzles.** Put the same
size back in that came out, or update the machine profile to match. The stock X1C hotend is
hardened steel; if a brass or
unknown hotend has been fitted, it does not print filled material until it is swapped back.

### Common failures

- **First-layer inspection fails** — dirty or wrong plate 90% of the time. Wipe with IPA,
  verify plate type in the slicer, restart.
- **Spaghetti detection triggers** — part came unstuck mid-print. Clear all debris from the
  bed and around the toolhead before restarting; check the plate prep and consider a brim.
- **AMS retry / feed error** — tangled spool or filament ground down by the feeder. Unload,
  trim the damaged section, check the spool for cross-winds, reload.
- **Clicking extruder / underextrusion on filled materials** — wet filament or worn nozzle. Dry
  the spool first; if it persists, the nozzle is due for replacement (weekly wear check is on
  the maintenance schedule).

## Cleanup & Maintenance

After your print:

- Remove parts **and** purge waste; leave the plate empty and clipped in.
- Return spools you unloaded to their dry box or bag.
- Wipe the plate with 90%+ IPA every few prints, or immediately after glue-stick use.

On the schedule (see [Maintenance Schedules](/maintenance/schedules/)):

- **Every few prints:** wipe plates with 90%+ IPA.
- **Weekly:** check nozzle wear on the CF printers (part of the farm walk-down).
- **Monthly:** clean or replace the nozzle wiper pads.
- **After any nozzle change:** full lidar + vibration calibration before the next job.
