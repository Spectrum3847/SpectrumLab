The Gweike Cloud M3 Ultra is a hybrid machine: a **1200W fiber laser and a 130W CO2 laser in one enclosure**, covering six processes — fiber metal cutting, welding, cleaning, rust removal, CO2 cutting, and CO2 engraving. One machine, two completely different lasers. The most important skill on this page is knowing which source you are firing and what it is allowed to touch.

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Two lasers, two hazard profiles — and one shared rule: lid down">
The fiber source (1064 nm, invisible) and the CO2 source (10,600 nm) behave completely differently on materials, so confirm which mode is active before every job. If you are not certain which laser will fire when you press start, stop and get a mentor.

Both are Class 4, and for both the thing protecting your eyes is the enclosure — the lid and window diffuse the beam to a safe level, which is why you can watch a job without eye protection. **Lid closed and interlocked whenever either source can fire.** Never run it open, never defeat an <Term id="interlock">interlock</Term>; a faulty interlock means the machine is down.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="NEVER cut PVC or vinyl">
Any chlorinated plastic — PVC, vinyl, most faux leather — releases chlorine gas when lasered. It will corrode the machine and hurt your lungs. There is no power setting that makes it safe. Unknown plastic = no cut until a mentor identifies it.
</SafetyWarning>

- Class 4 machine: the lid stays closed while firing and interlocks are never bypassed. That is the eye protection.
- Fiber mode on metal means reflections — same rules as the [xTool MetalFab](/lasers/xtool-metalfab/): no mirror-finish stock, material flat on the bed.
- Fire watch: stay with the machine for every job, know the extinguisher location, and keep the bed clear of offcut litter that can catch.
- Fume extraction on for everything — metal fumes in fiber mode, smoke and resin fumes in CO2 mode.
- Welding, cleaning, and rust-removal are open-beam handheld processes. **This lab does not use them. Ever** — same policy as the [MetalFab handheld module](/lasers/xtool-metalfab/#handheld-welding-module--we-do-not-use-it). Open-beam work is the one thing that would require wavelength-rated eyewear for everyone in the room, and we avoid it by keeping every job inside the enclosure.
- Loose sleeves and dangling lanyards stay away from the gantry. See [PPE & the glove policy](/safety/gloves-and-ppe/).

## Mode Selection

The 6-in-1 design means the machine does exactly what the active mode tells it to — so mode selection is step zero of every job:

1. Decide the process: **fiber** for metal cutting; **CO2** for wood, acrylic, and other approved non-metals. The handheld modes are not used here at all.
2. Select the mode in the control software and confirm the correct head/source is active before loading material.
3. Check the material against the matrix below for that mode.
4. When you finish, return the machine to its idle/default state so the next operator doesn't inherit a surprise.

## Material Compatibility Matrix

| Material | Mode | OK? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon steel (≤ 10 mm) | Fiber | Yes | Assist gas per job; oxide edge is normal with air |
| Stainless steel | Fiber | Yes | Nitrogen for clean edges |
| Aluminum | Fiber | Yes | Reflective — flat, non-polished stock only |
| Brass / copper | Fiber | Ask first | Highly reflective; mentor sets these jobs up |
| Plywood / solid wood | CO2 | Yes | Watch for internal glue voids flaring |
| Cast acrylic | CO2 | Yes | Beautiful flame-polished edges |
| MDF | CO2 | Yes | Chars; run high air assist |
| Leather (real) | CO2 | Yes | Smells terrible; extraction on high |
| Cardboard / paper | CO2 | Yes | Never unattended — catches easily |
| Carbon fiber plate | — | Mentor-only | Conductive dust and nasty fumes; requires sign-off |
| **PVC / vinyl / faux leather** | Any | **NEVER** | Chlorine gas — corrodes machine, poisons you |
| Polycarbonate | CO2 | Yes | Cuts fine — moderate power, moderate speed, high air assist |
| **ABS** | CO2 | **NEVER** | Melts into goo, toxic fumes |
| Metals | CO2 | No | CO2 will not cut metal — that is what fiber mode is for |

If it is not on this list, it does not go in the machine until a mentor identifies it. The same never-cut rules apply on the [Thunder Nova 35](/lasers/thunder-nova-35/).

## Rotary Setup (cylindrical engraving)

For engraving tumblers, tubes, and other cylinders in CO2 mode:

1. Power the machine off before plugging in or unplugging the rotary attachment.
2. Place the rotary on the bed, square to the gantry, and connect it to the rotary port.
3. Power on and **enable rotary mode in the software** — this remaps the Y axis to rotation. Forgetting this step scribbles your artwork around the cylinder.
4. Enter the workpiece diameter, load the cylinder, and confirm it turns freely without wobble.
5. Focus on the **top surface of the cylinder**, not the bed.
6. Run a low-power test frame or trace before the real engrave.
7. When done: disable rotary mode in software, power off, unplug, and store the rotary. Leaving rotary mode enabled ruins the next person's flat job.

## Water Cooling Checks

Both sources depend on the integrated water cooling loop. A cooked laser tube is the most expensive mistake you can make on this machine.

- Before starting: confirm the chiller/cooling loop is running and the coolant level is in range.
- **Never fire the laser with a flow or temperature alarm active.** An alarm is a hard stop, not a suggestion — power down and get a mentor.
- Coolant is distilled water only. Top-ups get logged on the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).
- If ambient temperature in the shop is unusually hot or cold, give the cooling loop extra attention mid-job.

## Operating Instructions

1. Power on the machine and fume extraction; verify the cooling loop is circulating.
2. Select the mode for your job and confirm the correct source is live before you load material.
3. Import your file in the control software, verify scale, and pick material settings — start from library presets and test on scrap for anything new.
4. Load the material flat on the slat bed. Fixture anything that could shift or curl.
5. Set focus for the active head per the station procedure.
6. Frame the job to confirm position and fit.
7. Close the lid and run. **Stay with the machine** — CO2 jobs can flare, fiber jobs throw sparks.
8. After the job, let extraction clear the enclosure before opening the lid.
9. Unload parts, clear the skeleton, and return the machine to its default mode.

## Our Experience

The Gweike is currently a **coach-run machine**. The goal is students running it solo, but it
needs more documentation first — the software has no step-by-step guide and it is easy to forget a
step. Things worth knowing:

- **No cameras.** You set the start point manually and use the **frame** feature to confirm the cut
  lands on the sheet. Frame every job.
- **Calibration drifts.** We have realigned the CO2 laser several times, and the steps are slightly
  off — parts have come out roughly **0.02" small over 8"**. Both are adjustable; flag it to a
  mentor rather than fudging dimensions in CAD.
- **Nicer pierce cleanup.** It cleans the metal after piercing so the nozzle avoids swarf, which
  means fewer mid-job pauses than the xTool.
- **Setup was involved** — you wire your own plug onto bare cables for both the laser box and the
  CNC. Both share one 220 V circuit through a splitter.
- **Connection is direct ethernet** to the machine, not over the network. The built-in nesting works
  but needs a USB key left in the computer.
- **Support runs through WhatsApp** — they add you to a group with their support staff and
  engineers. Convenient, if unusual.
- **Footprint:** 86" × 67" for the CNC, plus a foot or more behind it for exhaust, and 25" × 14" for
  the laser box beside it.

Materials, aluminum sourcing, workholding, and post-processing are shared with the xTool — see
[Fiber Laser Cutting — Materials & Practice](/lasers/fiber-laser-cutting/).

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Remove all material and offcuts; sweep debris off the slat bed.
- Return the machine to default mode, rotary disabled and unplugged.
- Let the cooling loop and extraction run their shutdown cycle before powering off.
- Wipe the lid glass so the next person can actually see their job.
- Log lens/nozzle condition and coolant level on the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).

For dedicated CO2 work with a full LightBurn settings library, use the [Thunder Nova 35](/lasers/thunder-nova-35/). For dedicated metal sheet cutting, the [xTool MetalFab](/lasers/xtool-metalfab/) is the primary machine.
