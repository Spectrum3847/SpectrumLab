This page is the shared knowledge for both fiber lasers — materials, sourcing, workholding, and
the habits that make cuts come out right. The machine-specific SOPs live on
[xTool MetalFab](/lasers/xtool-metalfab/) and [Gweike M3 Ultra](/lasers/gweike-m3-ultra/).

A fiber laser focuses light to a microscopic point to vaporize metal, with high-pressure air
blowing the melt out of the kerf. For us that means cutting a full mechanism out of aluminum in
under 30 minutes with no broken end mills, no hold-down screws or tabs, and parts that come off
the bed essentially ready to assemble.

## Which machine do I use?

Both machines are **1200 W** and cut aluminum to the same thickness. Pick by size, material, and
who is running it.

| | [xTool MetalFab](/lasers/xtool-metalfab/) | [Gweike M3 Ultra](/lasers/gweike-m3-ultra/) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Work area** | 24" × 24" | 54" × 36" — fits a full belly pan |
| **Laser type** | Fiber only → **metal only** | Fiber **+ CO2** → metal, wood, plastic, SRPP |
| **Alignment** | 16 MP camera matrix — drop a scrap anywhere and place parts on it | No cameras — set the origin manually and use the frame feature |
| **Software** | xTool Creative Space — guided, Bambu-Studio-like | Industrial, translated; more control, easier to skip a step |
| **Who runs it** | Students, solo after training | Coaches, or students with a coach |
| **Exhaust** | 8" | 6" |

Rules of thumb: **belly pans and full sheets go on the Gweike** because of the bed. **SRPP,
wood, and plastic go on the Gweike** because it is the only one with CO2. **Odd scraps go on the
xTool** because the cameras and sliding clamps make it trivial. When both are free and it is
plain aluminum, use the xTool — it is the easier machine.

## Materials & thicknesses

- **Thin aluminum cuts fast.** 0.040", 0.060", 0.090", and 0.125" all cut great on both machines.
  All of it is **5052** so we can bend it on the [Titan press brake](/forming/langmuir-titan-25t/).
- **Thick plate is 5 mm 7075.** That is what our thick structural plate is made from.
- **¼" is the practical ceiling** and we avoid it. We have cut ¼" 6061 on the xTool and it worked,
  but it is 20% heavier than 5 mm, more expensive, and we do not plan to run it regularly. 7075
  leaves a better finish than 6061.
- **Settings barely differ between alloys.** The xTool has presets for 6061 and 7075 and we just
  use them; the Gweike has no separate settings and cuts both the same.
- **Carbon fiber** will cut on the fiber lasers but it is sketchy and not something we do casually.
- **Steel** — we have only cut thin steel during machine bring-up. Thicker steel likely wants
  N2/O2 assist gas, which we do not run.

<SafetyWarning type="tip" title="Cut height is the setting you will actually fight">
Both machines need their cut height tuned to the specific material to keep cutting cleanly. **Too
close** and you get nozzle-contact warnings and the machine pauses — the xTool will sometimes
cancel the job outright. **Too far** and it does not cut through. If cuts start going bad, check
height before you change power or speed.
</SafetyWarning>

## Where our aluminum comes from

Aluminum pricing moves fast — do not assume these numbers still hold.

- **Thick plate (5 mm 7075):** imported from an Alibaba supplier as 600 × 900 mm (2' × 3') sheets,
  sized to feed through the xTool's passthrough brushes. About **$972 for 14 sheets** including
  shipping and tax.
- **Thin sheet (5052):** bought from a local supplier cut to 4' × 3' so it drops into the Gweike
  as a whole sheet.
- **Donated 6061 1/16"** arrives in 5' × 10' sheets.
- For the xTool, oversized sheets get cut down with the
  **[M12 nibbler](/portable-power/m12-ecosystem/)**.

## Workholding

- **xTool** — sliding sheet clamps. Easy to grab small and odd-shaped scraps, which is why the
  scrap pile lives next to it.
- **Gweike** — a standard slat bed. Sheets are not always flat, and small or odd pieces do not lay
  across the slats evenly. Plan for it: put small parts on the xTool.

## Catch trays

Both machines drop cut parts into a tray below the bed.

- On the **xTool** the tray is further from the bed, so the beam defocuses before it hits — less
  damage, and there is a pull-out drawer for collecting parts and scrap.
- On the **Gweike** the tray sits closer, so the bottom takes more damage over time.
- Both have sand in the bottom, though air assist blows it around. We are looking at rocks or
  another material instead.

## Post-processing

1. **Knock out the cutouts.** xTool tolerances are set tighter, so some need a punch; Gweike cuts
   run slightly looser and usually drop out if you tap the part over the recycling bin.
2. **Light sanding or deburring** — burrs are small most of the time.
3. **Wipe with acetone**, then most parts go to [powder coating](/finishing/powder-coating/).
4. **Bearing holes get modeled at bearing size.** No oversizing. Parts come out close enough, and
   we powder coat anyway and usually add bearing retention. Occasionally a bearing needs the
   [arbor press](/forming/arbor-press/) after powder, or a deburr.

The xTool holds about **0.001"** most of the time. The Gweike's steps have drifted slightly —
parts have come out roughly 0.02" small over 8" — and it is adjustable, so flag it to a mentor
rather than compensating in CAD.

## Shop services both machines depend on

- **Air:** the [Quincy QT-5 80-gallon, 130+ psi compressor](/portable-power/dewalt-and-pneumatics/)
  feeds both. Compressed air is the only assist gas we use, and it is enough for what we cut. No
  N2 or O2 — we are not getting school approval for gas cylinders.
- **Exhaust:** both machines tie into the whole-shop Donaldson cyclone dust collector, venting
  outside. **Never run either laser with the collector off.**

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Both machines pause when the nozzle touches metal">
A raised piece of swarf or a warped sheet will stop a job mid-cut. That is the machine protecting
itself, not a malfunction. Clear the swarf, check the sheet is flat, and restart — do not defeat
the warning.
</SafetyWarning>

## Experiments worth knowing about

- **Tube cutting** — the Gweike's edge detection plus its adjustable Z bed can cut tube. It is
  awkward and alignment-sensitive since you cut each face separately, but it built the metal Hub
  for our practice field in 2026.
- **Carbon fiber** — both will cut it with the fiber laser. Rarely worth it.
- **Laser welding and cleaning** (xTool) — we have never used these features and do not plan to.
