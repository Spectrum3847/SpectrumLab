We use it to cut aluminum and steel sheet fast and clean. It is the most dangerous laser in the lab: **Class 4, and its beam is invisible.**

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Class 4 fiber laser — invisible beam, retina hazard">
The 1064 nm fiber wavelength is invisible and passes straight through ordinary safety glasses and the front of your eye, focusing on the retina. A reflected flash can cause permanent blind spots before you can blink. Everyone in the area wears laser safety glasses rated for 1064 nm whenever the machine could fire outside a fully closed enclosure. Regular safety glasses do not count.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Reflective metal warning">
Aluminum, brass, and copper are mirrors at this wavelength. Never fire at polished or mirror-finish stock, never place shiny scrap under the head, and keep the material flat — a buckled sheet can redirect the beam. If a job needs mirror-finish material, talk to a mentor first.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Fire watch">
Fiber cutting throws sparks and hot dross. Keep the area clear of flammables and solvents, know where the fire extinguisher is, and stay with the machine for the entire job plus a few minutes after — hot dross can smolder in the scrap tray after the cut ends.
</SafetyWarning>

- Never bypass an enclosure interlock. If an interlock is faulty, the machine is down — tag it and tell a mentor.
- No rings, watches, or loose sleeves when loading and unloading — sheet metal edges and hot parts. See [PPE & the glove policy](/safety/gloves-and-ppe/).
- Cut parts and dross are hot. Give them a minute, or handle with pliers.
- Fume extraction runs for every cut. Metal cutting fumes are not something to breathe.

### Welding module — training gate

The MetalFab includes a handheld laser **welding module**. It exists, it works, and **it is off-limits without a separate mentor-only sign-off.** Handheld welding is an open-beam Class 4 process — the enclosure that protects you during cutting is not there. Do not pick up the torch, do not switch the machine to weld mode, do not "just try it." Ask a mentor about welding training if you want to learn.

## Software & Workflow

The station PC next to the machine runs the xTool MetalFab software (you can see it on the monitor by the blue toolbox).

1. **Export your part** as DXF (from Onshape/Fusion) or SVG. Flat profiles only — this is a 2D cutter.
2. **Import into the MetalFab software** and verify scale against a known dimension.
3. **Select material and thickness** from the preset library. The preset sets power, speed, and pierce parameters — start from the preset, not from scratch.
4. **Set assist gas type and pressure** to match the material (see below).
5. **Set the focal height** for your material thickness using the machine's focus procedure. Wrong focus is the number-one cause of dross and incomplete cuts — when a cut suddenly gets ugly, check focus before blaming power.
6. **Position the job** on the sheet, frame it to confirm it fits, close the enclosure, and run.

## Assist Gas Selection

The assist gas blows molten metal out of the kerf. Choosing it is part of the job setup:

| Gas | Use for | Edge result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Compressed air** | Carbon steel, general-purpose cuts, anything getting powder coated or painted | Slight oxide layer on the edge; cheap and always available |
| **Nitrogen** | Stainless and aluminum when you need a clean, oxide-free edge — especially parts that will be welded | Bright, weld-ready edge; costs real money per cylinder |

Default to compressed air. Switch to nitrogen only when the edge finish genuinely matters, and check the cylinder pressure before starting a long job — running out of gas mid-cut ruins the part.

## Consumables & Tooling

There is no consumables table for this machine yet — its wear items are reordered directly from [xTool support](https://support.xtool.com/product/56):

- **Protective lens (cover window):** sits below the focus lens and takes the spatter. Inspect before every session; a pitted or hazy window scatters the beam and kills cut quality. Keep spares on hand.
- **Cutting nozzles:** wear items. A dinged or dross-crusted nozzle distorts the gas flow — swap it, don't fight it.
- **Assist gas:** shop air is plumbed; nitrogen comes from cylinders. Note the level after use so the next person isn't surprised.

## Operating Instructions

1. Power on the machine and the fume extraction, and let the system finish its startup checks.
2. Load your sheet onto the cutting bed. Flat and supported — bridge any warp with fixturing, and confirm nothing shiny is sitting in the beam path.
3. Open the job in the MetalFab software, set material, gas, and focus as above.
4. Frame the job to confirm it fits your sheet with margin.
5. Close the enclosure. Confirm the interlock indicator is happy.
6. Start the cut and **stay with the machine.** Watch through the viewing window — with rated glasses on — for pierce failures, flare-ups from reflective spots, or dross buildup.
7. When the job ends, wait for fume extraction to clear the enclosure before opening.
8. Remove parts with care — edges are sharp and dross is hot. Drop metal skeletons and offcuts in the aluminum recycling bin if aluminum, scrap steel bin otherwise.
9. Check the scrap tray under the bed for hot dross before walking away.

## Our Experience

The MetalFab is the fiber laser students run **solo** after training — the software walks you
through each step and the cameras remove the hardest part of setup. Things worth knowing:

- **Camera alignment is the killer feature.** The 16 MP camera matrix means you drop an irregular
  scrap anywhere on the bed and place parts onto it visually. No edge-finding, no origin math.
- **The built-in nesting does not work well** in our experience. Lay parts out yourself.
- **The computer connects over USB**, and it has its own dedicated machine so the laser and other
  tools can be set up at the same time.
- **The laser module failed once**, about 2.5 months into use. Support sent a replacement after we
  paid a $4k deposit that was refunded when we shipped the broken one back — roughly 1.5 weeks of
  downtime. Worth knowing the failure mode exists; report anything odd early.

Materials, aluminum sourcing, workholding, and post-processing are shared with the Gweike — see
[Fiber Laser Cutting — Materials & Practice](/lasers/fiber-laser-cutting/).

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Power down in order: finish the job, let extraction run, then shut down the software, laser, and extraction.
- Close the assist gas cylinder valve after nitrogen jobs and note the remaining pressure.
- Empty the dross/scrap tray once it's cool. Sweep up metal skeleton offcuts.
- Wipe down the bed slats; heavy dross buildup on slats scars the underside of future cuts.
- Inspect the protective lens at the end of your session and log anything marginal on the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).

Need thicker aluminum, polycarbonate, or anything non-metal? That is the [WAZER Pro waterjet](/waterjet/wazer-pro/). Wood and acrylic belong on the [Thunder Nova 35](/lasers/thunder-nova-35/) or the CO2 side of the [Gweike M3 Ultra](/lasers/gweike-m3-ultra/).
