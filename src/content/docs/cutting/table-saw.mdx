The DeWalt jobsite table saw (DWE7491RS-class) is our rip-and-crosscut station for **wood and plastics** — field borders, plywood bases, practice-field elements, and crate parts. It lives on a rolling cart so it can move to the work. The table saw injures more people per year than any other tool in a typical shop, and nearly every one of those injuries follows the same script: no <Term id="riving-knife">riving knife</Term>, freehand feeding, or hands where a push stick should be. Our rules exist to break that script.

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="The riving knife stays ON">
The riving knife is the thin fin behind the blade that keeps the kerf from closing and pinching — pinching is what launches <Term id="kickback">kickback</Term>. It stays installed for every through-cut, period. If a cut genuinely can't run with the riving knife, that cut needs a mentor and a different plan, not a removed knife.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Push sticks ALWAYS within reach">
Two push sticks live at the saw and stay on the table, not in a drawer. Any rip narrower than about 6 in gets a push stick — your hand never passes within 6 in of the blade. If you find yourself deciding whether your fingers will clear, you already know the answer: push stick.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="No freehand cuts. No metal.">
Every cut is guided by the <Term id="fence">fence</Term> (rips) or the miter gauge (crosscuts) — never both at once on the same cut, and never neither. And this saw cuts **no metal, ever** — not aluminum, not "just a thin bit." Metal goes to the [chop saw](/cutting/evolution-saws/) or [bandsaws](/cutting/bandsaws/).
</SafetyWarning>

- Stand to the **side** of the blade line, not behind the stock — kickback fires the workpiece straight back.
- Wait for full blade speed before feeding, and push stock **past the blade** at the end of the cut — don't leave the offcut vibrating against the teeth.
- Never reach over or behind a spinning blade to grab a piece. Blade stopped first.
- No gloves at the saw ([glove policy](/safety/gloves-and-ppe/)); hearing protection on.

## Kickback: Causes & Prevention

Kickback happens when the stock rotates into the back of the blade or the <Term id="kerf">kerf</Term> pinches it, and the blade throws the piece at you at blade-rim speed.

| Cause | Prevention |
| --- | --- |
| Kerf closes and pinches the blade | Riving knife installed, always |
| Fence not parallel to blade (stock wedges) | Fence alignment check (below) |
| Freehand feeding or twisting the stock | Fence or miter gauge on every cut |
| Using miter gauge + fence together | One or the other — offcut trapped against the fence kicks |
| Wet, warped, or internally stressed wood | Flat face down; suspect boards get a mentor look first |
| Standing in the line of fire | Feed from beside the blade line |
| Letting go mid-cut | Feed through and past the blade, every time |

## Fence Alignment

The rack-and-pinion fence stays parallel by design, but verify after transport or a bump: measure from the fence to the same blade tooth at the front of the blade, rotate that tooth to the back, and measure again. Equal (or a hair wider at the back — never narrower) is correct. A fence that closes toward the back of the blade is a kickback machine — flag it and get a mentor before using the saw.

## Blade Height

Set the blade so the **teeth just clear the top of the stock** — about a tooth's height (roughly 1/8–1/4 in) above the material. Higher exposes more blade for no benefit; a barely-exposed blade also pushes the work down at the table instead of back at you.

## Operating Instructions

1. Set fence width (use the rack-and-pinion scale, verify with a tape for critical cuts) and blade height.
2. Stand beside the blade line, stock flat on the table and firm against the fence.
3. Start the saw, let it reach full speed.
4. Feed smoothly — steady pressure **forward and against the fence**. Swap to the push stick before your hand gets within 6 in of the blade.
5. Push the work completely past the blade; let the offcut sit until the blade stops.
6. Blade stopped → clear offcuts → next cut. Never clear the table around a spinning blade.

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Power off and unplug before any blade or throat-plate work.
- Vacuum sawdust from the table, fence rails, and under the saw — the DeWalt shop vac lives under the workbench.
- Lower the blade fully, coil the cord, roll the cart back to its parking spot, and lock the casters.
- Blade sharpness and fence-alignment checks are on the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).
