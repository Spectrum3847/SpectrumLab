## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Work on the DOWNWARD side, against the rest">
Always present the part where the belt or wheel is moving **downward / into the table and tool rest**, so the machine pushes the work down against support instead of throwing it up at your face. On the grinders that means below the wheel centerline against the rest; on the belt sanders, against the platen with the belt travel pressing the part onto the table.
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="Small parts: pliers or a jig — never bare fingers near the belt">
Anything smaller than roughly your palm gets held with locking pliers, vise-grips, or a simple wood/printed holding jig. A belt that snags a small part pulls it — and the fingertips pinching it — into the gap at the tool rest in milliseconds. If you can't grip it safely, clamp it to a carrier or ask a mentor for a jig.
</SafetyWarning>

- No gloves — a snagged glove is worse than a snagged fingertip. See [PPE & the glove policy](/safety/gloves-and-ppe/).
- Parts get **hot** fast, especially small aluminum. Dunk cup or wait — don't press harder because you can't feel it through the heat.
- Keep the tool-rest gap tight (about 1/8 in on grinders). A wide gap swallows parts and fingers.
- Never sand/grind on the unsupported side of a belt or the side of a stone wheel.
- Sparks from steel travel — check what's behind you (no solvent cans, no fuel, no people) before grinding.

## Aluminum vs the Wheels

**Aluminum never touches the stone (vitrified) grinding wheels.** Aluminum smears into the pores of a stone wheel — "loading" — and a loaded wheel can heat unevenly, go out of balance, and burst. Aluminum's tools are:

- **Belt sanders** — fine for aluminum; clean the belt with a crepe stick when it starts loading up (shiny, glazed streaks).
- **Scotch-Brite deburring wheels** — ideal for aluminum edge-break; the wheel sheds rather than loads.
- Stone wheels are for **steel only**: sharpening, weld cleanup, rough shaping.

If you spot silvery smears on a stone wheel, tag the grinder out and tell a mentor — the wheel needs dressing or replacement before anyone runs it.

## The Deburring Workflow

Every cut part — from the [chop saw](/cutting/evolution-saws/), [bandsaws](/cutting/bandsaws/), lathe, or shear — follows the same 60-second routine before it goes in the parts bin:

1. **Flats and outside edges:** a few light passes on the 4 in horizontal belt to remove saw marks and square up if needed.
2. **Edge break:** roll every cut edge lightly against the Scotch-Brite wheel — two or three passes per edge, light pressure, keep the part moving. You're removing the burr, not changing the dimension.
3. **Inside curves and slots:** the 1 in vertical belt reaches where the wheel can't; hand deburring tools (red toolbox, Pliers & Cutters drawer) finish holes.
4. **Test with a thumb-drag** (gently, along — not across — the edge). If it would snag a wire's insulation or a teammate's hand, it's not done.

## Technique Notes

- **Light pressure, keep it moving.** Pressure makes heat, not speed; dwelling makes divots and bluing.
- Grind/sand so the contact point stays on the tool rest or table — floating work chatters and grabs.
- On the horizontal belt, square ends against the miter fence if fitted; check with a square, not eyeballs.
- Long stock: mind the far end — it sweeps behind you as you rotate the part.

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Power off and let wheels spin down before walking away.
- Brush dust and swarf off the tables and rests; sweep or vacuum the bench — mixed aluminum/steel dust doesn't go in the recycling bin.
- Report torn belts, glazed or loaded wheels, and any wheel that vibrates on startup — wheel dressing and replacement is mentor work, tracked on the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/).
