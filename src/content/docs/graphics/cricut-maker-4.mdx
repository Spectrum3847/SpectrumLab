The Cricut Maker 4 is our precision blade cutter. It handles 300+ materials with 13 swappable tools, and it's what we use for adhesive vinyl decals, heat-transfer vinyl (HTV) for team shirts, stencils, cardstock, and die-cut stickers. Cut HTV here, then press it with the [heat press](/graphics/heat-press/); finishing workflows live on [Vinyl & Sticker Finishing](/graphics/vinyl-and-stickers/).

## Safety

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="The blades are scalpels">
Handle blade housings by the barrel, never the tip. When swapping blades out of a housing, push the plunger and keep your fingers off the cutting edge. Weeding tools are sharp too — drag them, don't stab toward your hand.
</SafetyWarning>

- **Do** keep fingers, hair, and sleeves away from the rollers while the machine is cutting.
- **Do** cap or dock blades when they're out of the machine — loose blades in the drawer cut the next person.
- **Don't** force a jammed mat; unload it from Design Space or the machine button.
- **Don't** cut materials the machine isn't rated for — when in doubt, check Cricut's material list or ask a lead.

## Software & Workflow

### Design Space setup and pairing

1. Install **Cricut Design Space** from [cricut.com/setup](https://cricut.com/setup) and sign in — ask a graphics lead which account the team uses.
2. Power on the Maker 4. Connect one of two ways:
   - **USB:** plug the cable into the graphics computer. Done.
   - **Bluetooth:** open your computer's Bluetooth settings, pair with the Maker 4, then let Design Space finish machine setup.
3. In Design Space, your machine appears in the connection dropdown on the **Make It** screen. If it doesn't, check the cable/pairing before restarting anything else.

### Standard cut workflow

1. Design on the Canvas (or import an SVG). Size everything at final dimensions.
2. Click **Make It**. Confirm the layout preview — and turn **Mirror ON for every HTV cut** (you cut the back of the material).
3. Select the **base material** (Premium Vinyl, Everyday Iron-On, Cardstock, etc.). This is what sets cut pressure — there is no dial on the machine; material selection *is* the pressure setting. Use the **More/Less pressure** dropdown for fine-tuning: "More" helps when a blade is getting dull.
4. Load material (see mats vs Smart Materials below), press the flashing load button, then **Go**.
5. Unload, then peel the **mat away from the material** (flip it and bend the mat), not the material off the mat — this keeps vinyl and cardstock from curling.

Run a small **test cut** (a 1-inch shape in a corner) any time you use a new material or a fresh blade. A good cut goes through the vinyl but only kisses the backing liner.

### Mats: match the grip to the material

| Mat | Color | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LightGrip | Blue | Paper, cardstock, printable sticker paper |
| StandardGrip | Green | Adhesive vinyl, HTV/iron-on, heavier cardstock — the default |
| StrongGrip | Purple | Thick stuff: chipboard, leather, glitter cardstock, magnet |

A mat that's too sticky tears paper on removal; a mat that's too worn lets vinyl shift mid-cut and ruins the job. Retire mats that no longer hold a corner down.

### Smart Materials (matless cutting)

Smart Vinyl, Smart Iron-On, and Smart Paper have a full-width stiff liner, so they feed **directly into the machine with no mat**. Design Space offers "Without mat" on the Make It screen when the material supports it. This is how you cut long, banner-length designs that no 12" mat could hold — just keep the path behind the machine clear, because the material passes back and forth through the rear during the cut.

### Blades: Fine-Point vs Deep-Point

- **Fine-Point (45°):** the default blade. Vinyl, HTV, paper, cardstock — 95% of what we cut.
- **Deep-Point (60°):** steeper, more aggressive blade for thick materials: magnet sheet, chipboard, foam sheet, stiffened felt.

**Housing swap** takes ten seconds:

1. Open **Clamp B** on the carriage.
2. Pull the current blade housing straight up and out.
3. Drop the other housing in — it self-seats, no adjustment.
4. Close the clamp. Make sure the material you selected in Design Space matches the blade you installed; Design Space will warn you if it expects a different tool.

Blades live in the Cricut drawer at the graphics bench. Replacement Fine-Point blades drop into the existing housing (push the plunger, swap, done).

## Operating Instructions

### Weeding technique

Weeding is removing everything that isn't your design, leaving it on the liner (vinyl) or carrier (HTV).

1. Grab the weeding tools from the Cricut drawer — the hook does most of the work; tweezers rescue small pieces.
2. **Start from a corner** of the waste area and peel the excess back at a sharp, low angle. Slow and steady; ripping fast lifts your design with it.
3. Pull out the centers of letters (the "counters" — inside of o, a, e) with the hook tip.
4. Good light matters. Angle the cut toward a lamp so cut lines catch the light, or lightly flex the sheet to make them pop.
5. Ball up the weeded scrap and bin it immediately — vinyl scrap sticks to everything in the lab.

**Reverse weeding for intricate designs:** if tiny text or fine detail keeps lifting with the waste, don't fight it. Apply transfer tape over the whole *unweeded* cut, burnish it hard, flip the sandwich over and peel away the vinyl's backing liner — then weed the waste off the transfer tape from the sticky side. Small pieces stay anchored to the tape instead of tenting up.

### Transfer tape workflow (adhesive vinyl)

Transfer tape moves a weeded adhesive-vinyl design from its liner onto the final surface. (HTV never gets transfer tape — its clear carrier does that job. If you're doing HTV, go to the [heat press page](/graphics/heat-press/).)

1. Cut a piece of transfer tape slightly larger than the design and lay it over the weeded vinyl.
2. Burnish hard with a scraper — every letter, every edge.
3. Flip it over and peel the vinyl's paper liner off the back at a 45° angle, leaving the design on the tape. If a piece stays on the liner, lay it back down and burnish that spot again.
4. Clean the target surface with isopropyl alcohol and let it dry.
5. Position and lay the design down — for anything big, use the **hinge method**: tape one edge in place, fold back, peel the liner progressively, and squeegee outward from the hinge to chase out bubbles.
6. Burnish onto the surface, then peel the transfer tape back at a sharp angle, slowly. If vinyl lifts, stop, burnish, retry.

### Print-Then-Cut (for stickers)

Print-Then-Cut prints your artwork with a black registration box, then the Maker 4's sensor reads the marks and cuts precisely around each shape.

1. In Design Space, build your art as **printable** images (or Flatten your layers), then **Make It** → Design Space sends the sheet to a printer with registration marks added.
2. Print on printable sticker paper — matte works best; glossy sheets can confuse the registration sensor. The Maker 4 handles Print-Then-Cut sheets up to 24 × 28 in; big sheets print through the [plotter's sheet feeder](/graphics/hp-t630-plotter/#using-the-auto-sheet-feeder).
3. Stick the printed sheet to a LightGrip (blue) mat, aligned with the top-left grid corner.
4. Load the mat and press Go. The machine scans the registration box first, then cuts.
5. For a **kiss cut** sticker sheet (cut through the vinyl only, sheet stays whole), pick a sticker-sheet material setting; for fully die-cut stickers, pick a cut-through setting.

Full sticker recipes — including optional lamination — are on [Vinyl & Sticker Finishing](/graphics/vinyl-and-stickers/#die-cut-stickers).

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Unload your mat, put the cover liner back on it, and store it **flat** in the mat rack — curled mats cut crooked.
- Mats losing grip: lint-roll them, or wash with cool water and dish soap, air dry sticky-side up. Retire them when that stops working.
- Return blade housings to the Cricut drawer, blades capped.
- Ball up and trash all vinyl scrap; wipe stray adhesive off the bench.
- Power the machine off and leave the graphics bench clear for the next person.
- Blade life and mat condition are tracked on the [Maintenance Schedules](/maintenance/schedules/) page.
