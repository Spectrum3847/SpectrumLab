The HP DesignJet T630 is our 24-inch large-format inkjet plotter. We use it for full-size engineering drawings, 1:1 drill templates, field layout prints, team banners, and large sticker sheets for [Print-Then-Cut](/graphics/cricut-maker-4/) jobs. It prints a D-size sheet in about 30 seconds and feeds from either a 24" roll or the auto sheet feeder — you pick the source per job, no re-rigging required.

## Safety

The plotter is one of the safest machines in the lab, but it is not zero-risk.

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Keep hands out of a moving printer">
The carriage moves fast and the built-in cutter blade is sharp. Never reach into the paper path or the window while a job is running. If something goes wrong, cancel the job from the front panel first, then open it up.
</SafetyWarning>

- **Do** power the printer off before clearing a jam.
- **Do** get help lifting full paper rolls — awkward, not heavy.
- **Don't** cut the roll edge with a blade while it's on the printer. Pull the roll, trim it square on a table.
- **Don't** lean drawings, banners, or your backpack against the stand — the media bin needs to stay clear so prints drop cleanly.

## Software & Workflow

There are two ways to print. Use **HP Click** for quick prints of finished files; use the **printer driver** when you're printing from CAD and need exact scale control.

### HP Click (drag-and-drop)

1. Open HP Click on the graphics computer. It auto-discovers the T630 on the lab network.
2. Drag your file into the window — PDF, JPEG, TIFF, and HP-GL/2 all work.
3. Check the live preview: it shows exactly how the job lands on the loaded media, including rotation.
4. Pick your media source (roll or sheet) and quantity, then hit **Print**. That's it — HP Click handles sizing sanely for one-off prints.

### Driver printing from CAD

For engineering drawings out of Onshape, SolidWorks, or Fusion, export the drawing as a **PDF at its real sheet size** (ANSI D / Arch D for full-size), then print through the HP driver:

1. Open the PDF and choose **Print** → HP DesignJet T630.
2. In printer properties, set the **document size** to match the drawing sheet and pick the **paper source** (roll for big sheets, sheet feeder for cut sheets).
3. Set scale to **Actual size / 100%** — never "fit to page" — when the print will be used as a template or measured against parts.
4. Print, then verify scale with calipers against a known dimension block on the drawing before you drill anything from it.

### Print queue via the embedded web server

The T630 runs a built-in web server you can reach from any computer on the lab network:

1. On the front panel, tap the network icon to read the printer's IP address.
2. Browse to `http://<printer-ip>`.
3. From there you can watch and cancel queued jobs, check ink levels and media status, and see usage history. If someone's 12-copy mistake is eating the roll, this is where you kill it.

## Consumables & Ink

The T630 takes four **HP 712** cartridges (cyan, magenta, yellow, black). Black is available as a standard 38-ml cartridge or the 80-ml **712XL** — buy the XL, it's what we stock. Paper is generic 24" 20-lb bond on a 2" core.

### Replacing a 712 / 712XL cartridge

Replace a cartridge when the front panel flags it — don't wait for a mid-banner failure.

1. Make sure no job is printing. On the front panel, tap the **ink icon** → **Replace ink cartridges**.
2. Open the ink cartridge cover.
3. Grip the depleted cartridge and pull it straight out.
4. Unwrap the new cartridge, match the color label to the slot, and push it in until it clicks seated.
5. Close the cover. The printer runs a brief ink check on its own.
6. Note the swap on the ink shelf so the next order goes in before we're dry — see [ordering more](/graphics/vinyl-and-stickers/#ordering-more).

## Operating Instructions

### Loading a roll

1. On the front panel, tap the paper icon → **Load roll**, and pull the old spindle out of the printer.
2. Pop the blue hub off the spindle and slide the old core off.
3. Slide the new roll on so the leading edge comes **over the top of the roll toward you**, then snug the blue hub back against the roll face — no side-to-side play.
4. Seat the spindle back in the printer, both ends clicked in.
5. Feed the leading edge into the roll slot until the printer grabs it. It aligns the paper and trims the edge square automatically.
6. Confirm the paper type and width on the front panel.

If the roll edge is torn or ragged, trim it square with scissors before feeding — a bad edge is the number one cause of load failures and skewed prints.

### Using the auto sheet feeder

For letter/tabloid/cut-sheet jobs, skip the roll entirely:

1. Fan the sheet stack and square it up.
2. Load the sheets into the auto sheet feeder and slide the guides against the stack — snug, not clamping.
3. Choose **Sheet** as the paper source in the driver or HP Click. The printer switches between roll and feeder per job on its own; you never unload the roll to print sheets.

### Banners and engineering drawings

- **Engineering drawings:** print from the roll at 100% scale on bond. Full-size drawings taped to stock make excellent drill/cut templates — verify one dimension with calipers first.
- **Banners:** design at final size, print from the roll using a custom paper size in HP Click or the driver, and the printer cuts the length automatically. Finishing (trimming and grommets) is covered in [Vinyl & Sticker Finishing](/graphics/vinyl-and-stickers/#team-banners).
- **Sticker sheets:** the sheet feeder also takes printable sticker media for large [Print-Then-Cut](/graphics/cricut-maker-4/#print-then-cut-for-stickers) jobs on the Cricut.

### Clearing a paper jam

1. Read the front panel message — it tells you where the jam is.
2. Power the printer **off**.
3. Open the window. If the carriage moves freely, slide it gently out of the way.
4. Pull the jammed paper out slowly in the direction it feeds. Remove **every** scrap — a leftover corner causes the next jam.
5. Check the roll input and the sheet feeder path for torn pieces.
6. Power back on and reload the media. The printer re-initializes on its own.
7. Repeat jams usually mean a ragged roll edge or an overfilled sheet feeder — fix the cause, not just the jam.

## Cleanup & Maintenance

- Leave the printer **powered on** in standby — it runs automatic printhead maintenance and wastes less ink than repeated cold starts.
- Take your prints with you; don't let the media bin pile up.
- Keep partial rolls in the graphics cabinet, on end, out of direct sun.
- Wipe dust off the top cover occasionally; never spray cleaner into the printer.
- Ink swaps and any error codes go in the log — see [Maintenance Schedules](/maintenance/schedules/).
