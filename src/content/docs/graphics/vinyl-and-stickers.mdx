| --- | --- |

## Die-Cut Stickers

1. **Design** at final size in Design Space (or import art and Flatten it into a printable image). Add a small bleed — extend background color slightly past the cut line so a hair of cut drift doesn't leave a white sliver.
2. **Print-Then-Cut:** Design Space prints the sheet with a registration box, the Maker 4 scans it and cuts. Use matte printable sticker paper — glossy can confuse the registration sensor. Letter-size sheets go through a desktop path; large sheets (the Maker 4 handles up to 24 × 28 in) print via the [plotter's sheet feeder](/graphics/hp-t630-plotter/#using-the-auto-sheet-feeder).
3. **Choose the cut style:** kiss cut (through the vinyl only — you get a peelable sticker sheet) or full die-cut (individual stickers cut all the way through).
4. **Laminate — optional but worth it** for stickers that live on water bottles or robot carts: lay clear laminate film over the printed sheet *before* cutting, squeegee out the bubbles, and pick a thicker material setting so the blade goes through both layers. Unlaminated inkjet stickers smear when wet.

## Team Banners

1. **Design at final size.** Banners read from distance — big text, high contrast, team colors. Export as PDF.
2. **Print from the roll** on the [HP T630](/graphics/hp-t630-plotter/): set a custom paper size in HP Click or the driver, and the printer feeds and cuts the length automatically off the 24" roll.
3. **Trim** the edges square with a straightedge and a sharp blade on a cutting mat — never freehand scissors on a display piece.
4. **Grommets:** fold or reinforce each corner (clear packing tape on the back works on paper banners), punch, and set a grommet in each corner and roughly every 2 feet along the top edge. Ask a graphics lead for the grommet kit if you can't find it.
5. Hang with zip ties or ball bungees through the grommets — never tape through the print face.

For banners that must survive outdoors or a full season of competitions, print on heavier media and laminate, or build the design from adhesive vinyl on a blank banner instead of printing — ask a lead which the event calls for.

## Apparel Heat Transfers

The full chain for shirts, hoodies, and safety-glasses bags — anything fabric.

<SafetyWarning type="tip" title="Mirror. Every. Time.">
HTV is cut from the back, so the design must be **mirrored at the Make It screen** and the vinyl loaded **shiny (carrier) side down**. An unmirrored cut is only discovered at the press, after the vinyl is already spent. The consumables label on the HTV rack says it too: cut mirrored, shiny side down.
</SafetyWarning>

1. **Design** one color per layer. Text must be bold enough to weed — hairline fonts become confetti.
2. **Cut** on the [Cricut Maker 4](/graphics/cricut-maker-4/): Everyday Iron-On / EasyWeed material setting, Mirror ON, shiny side down on a StandardGrip (green) mat or fed as Smart Iron-On.
3. **Weed** the excess, leaving the design on its clear carrier — technique on the [Cricut page](/graphics/cricut-maker-4/#weeding-technique). No transfer tape for HTV; the carrier is the transfer sheet.
4. **Press** on the [heat press](/graphics/heat-press/): pre-press the blank 3–5 s, then 305 °F / 15 s / medium pressure as the starting point for EasyWeed, cover sheet on top, hot peel.
5. **Aftercare:** wait 24 hours before the first wash; wash inside-out, cold; no ironing directly on the vinyl. Put that on the care tag note when you hand shirts out.

Sublimation and Infusible Ink apparel follow the same chain with different settings and poly-blend blanks — see the [heat press settings table](/graphics/heat-press/#settings--starting-points-not-gospel).
