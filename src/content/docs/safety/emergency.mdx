Read this page before you need it. In an emergency you will not think clearly — you will do whatever you rehearsed. The pattern for everything below is the same: **make it safe, get help, then deal with the details.**

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="When in doubt, call 911 first">
Serious bleeding, burns bigger than a palm, anything involving eyes, loss of consciousness, electric shock, or a fire you can't knock down instantly: **call 911 immediately.** Do not wait for a mentor's permission to call — mentors will always back that call. Then alert the nearest mentor.
</SafetyWarning>

## Injury Response

1. **Stop the machines.** Hit the <Term id="e-stop">E-stop</Term> on the machine involved; shut down neighboring machines so nobody else is distracted or endangered. Don't try to retrieve anything from a running machine.
2. **Shout for a mentor.** Say what and where: "Injury at the lathe!" A mentor takes charge from this point.
3. **Don't move a seriously injured person** unless they're in immediate danger (fire, machine still engaged).
4. **First aid:** the kit is in the **First Aid drawer of the blue Durham cabinet**. The daily opening checklist verifies it's stocked and in place. Minor cuts and burns: clean, cover, and *still tell a mentor* — every injury gets reported (see below).
5. For anything beyond a simple bandage, a mentor decides on 911 / urgent care / parent contact. When in doubt, escalate.

## Fire

1. **Yell "FIRE"** so everyone in the shop knows immediately.
2. **Small, contained fire** (trash-can sized, not spreading, not blocking your exit): one person grabs the nearest fire extinguisher — locations are marked in the shop [confirm locations] and verified daily on the opening checklist — and uses **PASS**: **P**ull the pin, **A**im at the base, **S**queeze, **S**weep. Everyone else clears back.
3. **Evacuate instead of fighting the fire if:** it's bigger than a trash can, it's spreading, smoke is filling the room, it involves the compressed-gas or electrical panels, or you have *any* doubt. Machines and robots are replaceable.
4. Evacuating: shut machines down with E-stops **only if it costs zero seconds**, get out, close doors behind you, meet at the assembly point [confirm assembly point], and call 911 from outside. A mentor takes a headcount.
5. Solvents and other flammables live in the green flammables cabinet in the storage room — keeping them there when not in use is fire prevention, not tidiness.

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Special fire cases in this shop">

- **Powder room:** airborne powder is combustible dust — the no-flame rule in the [Powder Coating SOP](/finishing/powder-coating/) exists to prevent this fire, because a dust flash spreads too fast to fight.
- **Lithium batteries (M12/DeWalt packs):** a hissing, smoking, or swelling pack cannot be extinguished by normal means. Do not fight it up close — evacuate and call 911. If it's small and just starting, moving it outside with pliers/shovel to open ground is a mentor's call only.
- **Laser cutters:** small flare-ups inside the machine happen; that's what the machine's own procedures cover. A fire that persists after the laser stops is a real fire — treat it as above.

</SafetyWarning>

## Power Shut-Offs

Know your three levels, fastest to slowest:

1. **Machine E-stop** — the big red button on every CNC machine and major tool. Stops that machine instantly. E-stops are tested monthly per the [maintenance schedule](/maintenance/schedules/), so they will work when you hit them. Hit first, ask questions later — an unnecessary E-stop costs nothing.
2. **Machine power switch** — every machine's main switch. The closing checklist powers everything down here nightly.
3. **Shop power cut-off** — the full-shop power cut-off on the wall near the vacuum light switch. Kills power to the whole shop, including a machine whose E-stop or switch is unreachable or has failed, and anything electrical that is smoking. If you cut shop power in an emergency, leave it off until a mentor clears the machine.

Also know: the **shop air compressor shutoff and line drain** (for a burst line or fitting — the hiss is loud but the fix is just shutting the valve), and that unplugging a benchtop tool is a perfectly good E-stop.

## Eyes

Anything in the eye — chips, solvent splash, powder — gets flushed **immediately and continuously for 15 minutes**. The lab has **no plumbed eye-wash station**, so go to the nearest sink and flush with clean running water; have someone else call a mentor while you keep flushing. Do not rub, and do not try to pick anything out of an eye. Chemical exposure or anything embedded: flush and call 911 / head to the ER — eyes do not get a wait-and-see.

## Who to Call

| Situation | Call |
| --- | --- |
| Serious injury, fire, chemical exposure | **911**, then nearest mentor |
| Any injury, near-miss, or damaged machine | Nearest mentor, immediately |
| No mentor physically present | Stop work entirely — students don't run machines without a mentor in the shop — and call the lead mentor [confirm phone list location; post it by the door] |
| Building issues (power outage, water, alarms) | Mentor, who contacts the building contact [confirm contact] |

## Incident Reporting

Every injury and every near-miss gets written up — same day, no exceptions, no blame. A near-miss report is a free lesson; the report you skip is the one that lets it happen again to someone else.

Include: who, what machine/tool, what happened, what PPE was in use, and what would have prevented it. File it with a mentor [confirm reporting form/location]. Mentors review reports and turn them into fixes — guarding, training, or updates to these docs.

<SafetyWarning type="tip" title="The culture rule">
Nobody at Spectrum ever gets in trouble for stopping a machine, hitting an E-stop, calling 911, or reporting their own mistake. People get in trouble for hiding problems. When something feels wrong — stop, ask, report.
</SafetyWarning>
