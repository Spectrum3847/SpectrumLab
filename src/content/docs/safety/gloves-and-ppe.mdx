PPE in this shop is not a vibe, it's a system: each item protects against a specific hazard, and wearing the *wrong* PPE can be worse than wearing none. This page is the whole system. Learn the glove policy first — it's the one rule people get backwards.

## The Glove Policy

Gloves are the only piece of PPE in this shop that is **mandatory at some stations and banned at others**. Here is the entire policy:

> **Gloves protect you from what you touch.
> Gloves get you pulled into what spins.**

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="NO GLOVES ON ANY ROTATING MACHINERY — EVER">
**Lathe. Mill. Drill press. Bandsaws. Bench grinder. CNC router. Rotary tools.** If any part of the machine spins, your hands are bare.

A spinning tool that snags bare skin takes some skin and lets go. A spinning tool that snags a **glove** doesn't let go — it winds the glove up in a fraction of a second and pulls your hand, then your arm, into the machine. The glove that was supposed to protect you becomes the rope that feeds you in. This is one of the most common ways machinists lose fingers, and it is 100% preventable: **bare hands at anything that spins.**
</SafetyWarning>

<SafetyWarning type="critical" title="The ban includes 'just for a second'">
Walking from the powder booth to the drill press with gloves still on is exactly how this goes wrong. **Gloves come off before you approach a rotating machine** — not after you get there, not "just to line up the part." Same logic applies to anything else that dangles: sleeves, lanyards, hoodie strings, jewelry, hair. If it can wrap, it comes off or gets tied back.
</SafetyWarning>

### When gloves are REQUIRED

| Task | Gloves |
| --- | --- |
| Handling powder, spraying, powder cleanup | Nitrile |
| Solvents, degreasers, adhesives, cutting fluid | Nitrile |
| Sheet metal, freshly cut edges, sheared blanks | Cut-resistant or leather |
| Hot parts — curing oven, heat press, fresh laser/plasma cuts | Heat-resistant gloves |
| Handling prepped parts before coating | Nitrile ([skin oils ruin adhesion](/finishing/surface-prep/)) |

### When gloves are BANNED

| Machine | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Lathe (PM-1130V) | Chuck and workpiece spin — glove wrap = hand in the chuck |
| Mill | Spinning cutter and rotating spindle |
| Drill press | The classic glove-grabber — bit snags glove, spins your hand |
| Bandsaws (vertical, horizontal, portaband) | Moving blade drags glove and hand into the cut |
| Bench grinder / belt sander | Wheel grabs glove, feeds fingers into the rest |
| CNC router (ShopSabre) | Spindle — and never reach into a running machine anyway |
| Rotary/die grinder tools | Small, fast, and close to your fingers |

Nitrile gloves and safety glasses live in the top drawer of the red toolbox. There is no "we were out of gloves" excuse.

## Eye Protection

**Safety glasses are on from the moment you enter the machine shop.** Not when you start cutting — when you walk in. You don't control what the person at the next machine launches. Specifics:

- Regular prescription glasses are not safety glasses. Wear over-glasses safety goggles from the drawer, or get ANSI Z87+ prescription safety glasses.
- Face shield **over** (not instead of) safety glasses for grinding and wire-wheel work.
- Laser work has its own rules — see the individual laser pages for when laser safety glasses are required.

## Hearing Protection

Hearing damage is cumulative and permanent, and shop noise is louder than it feels. **Hearing protection goes on whenever loud tools are running** — yours or anyone else's: chop saws, grinders, the router, air tools, hammering. Earmuffs and plugs are stocked in the shop. If you have to raise your voice to talk to the person next to you, it's loud enough to need protection.

## Respirators

**Particulate respirators are required for powder coating** — any time powder is airborne: spraying, pouring, or cleanup. Booth respirators live on the shelf in the powder room; the full rules are in the [Powder Coating SOP](/finishing/powder-coating/). Dust masks are also the right call for long sanding sessions and MDF work.

## Clothing Standards

Every item here exists because of a real injury somewhere:

- **Closed-toe shoes, always.** Dropped stock and chips don't negotiate with sandals.
- **Tie back long hair** before operating or standing near any rotating machine. Hair wraps exactly like a glove does.
- **No loose sleeves.** Roll them up or wear short sleeves at machines.
- **No lanyards, hoodie strings, scarves, or dangling badges** at machines. Tuck or remove.
- **No rings, bracelets, or dangling jewelry.** Rings are their own horror category — a ring caught on moving equipment can deglove a finger. Take them off at the door.
- Aprons are available for messy work (powder, solvents, oiling machines) — an apron is for your clothes, not a substitute for any of the above.

## The Two-Second Check

Before you power on any machine, run this in your head:

1. **Eyes** — glasses on?
2. **Ears** — is this (or anything nearby) loud?
3. **Hands** — does this machine spin? Then gloves OFF. Handling something sharp, hot, or chemical? Then gloves ON.
4. **Dangles** — hair, sleeves, strings, jewelry secured?

Four checks, two seconds, every time. If anything is off, fix it before the switch flips.
