Machines don't fail at convenient times — they fail during build season, at 9 PM, three days before ship. The maintenance program below is how we stay ahead of that: daily checklists anyone can run, weekly and monthly preventive maintenance (PM), per-machine service intervals, and the reference for which cleaner and which lubricant goes where.

Everyone runs the daily lists. Weekly and monthly tasks are coordinated by the shop captain and mentors — but if you see something on these lists that needs doing, say so.

## Weekly preventive maintenance

- **WAZER**: check tank abrasive level, drain used-abrasive buckets if more than half full
- **Laser optics**: inspect (clean only if hazy) on the Nova 35, the MetalFab and the Gweike; empty debris trays
- Blow dust out of powder booth filters; replace the box-fan filter if caked
- **Printer farm**: wash PEI plates and sheets with Dawn and warm water every few prints; check nozzle wear on machines running filled filament
- Charge all M12/DeWalt batteries; report any that won't hold charge or get hot on the charger
- **Bandsaws**: brush sawdust and chips off wheels, tires and guides
- **Shop vacs**: check filters; rinse and fully dry the DeWalt filter after any wet pickup
- Restock small-parts bins from bulk stock; note anything below its reorder point in the [Consumables Ledger](/inventory/consumables/)

## Monthly

- **WAZER**: full maintenance per the maintenance manual (nozzle, mixing tube, tank clean-out)
- **Lathe**: oil ways and leadscrew, check belt tension, wipe and re-oil the chuck
- **Bandsaws**: check blade tension, tracking, and tire condition; redress tires only if glazed
- **Press brake**: grease ram guides; verify backgauge calibration
- **Curing oven**: inspect door seal and thermocouple reading vs. a reference thermometer
- **Table saw**: check fence parallelism and riving-knife alignment
- **Compressor**: check oil level in the Quincy sight glass; drain both tanks fully
- Test emergency stops on every CNC machine
- Inventory pass on the [Consumables Ledger](/inventory/consumables/) — reorder anything below minimum

## Seasonal / annual

Run these at kickoff and again at the end of the competition season:

- **Full toolbox audit.** Open every drawer of the [red](/storage/toolboxes/#red-toolbox--hand-tools) and [orange](/storage/toolboxes/#orange-toolbox--metrology-machinist-tooling--ppe) chests against its map on this site. Empty sawtooth slots mean a missing tool: find it or order it. Fix any label that no longer matches its drawer.
- **Pit kit restock.** Every T-Stak pit kit gets opened, inventoried, and closed full — before it goes back on the stack, not the night before an event.
- **Compressor oil.** Quincy QT-series oil is changed after the first 100 hours of a new machine and then **every 500 hours or once a year, whichever comes first**, using Quin-Cip-D (SAE 30 / ISO 100). Check the air filter at the same time.
- **Measuring tool check.** Verify each caliper and micrometer against a gauge block or standard. Anything that has been dropped gets checked or retired.
- **Blade and wheel sweep.** Retire dull saw blades, glazed sanding belts, and any stone wheel with aluminum smeared into it.
- **E-stop and guard walk-down.** Every guard present and moving freely, every E-stop tested, every machine's power switch reachable.
- **Battery cull.** Any M12/DeWalt pack that won't hold a charge comes out of the pool and gets recycled, not shuffled back in.

### [Bandsaws](/cutting/bandsaws/)

| Task | Interval |
|---|---|
| Brush chips and dust off wheels, tires, table and guides | Weekly |
| Check blade tension — about 1/4" deflection at moderate finger pressure mid-span | Monthly and after any blade change |
| Check blade tracking (mentor job) | Monthly |
| Release blade tension if the saw will sit unused for a long break | Per break |
| Redress glazed rubber tires with 100-grit, only enough to expose fresh rubber | As needed (mentor) |
| Replace blades that are cracked at the gullets, missing teeth, or cutting banana-shaped | On sight |

Over-tensioning is the classic G0555 mistake: it damages the upper hinge assembly, wears tires prematurely, and breaks blades. Tension to spec, not to maximum.

## Cleaning & Lubrication Reference

Which product, for what. This is Spectrum's own long-standing practice, condensed from the team's [Maintenance Guide](https://spectrum3847.org/resources/maintenance-guide/) — read that for the robot-side version (bumpers, electrical, and post-event deep cleans).

### Cleaning

| Job | Use | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| General degreasing, chain and gears, drivetrain parts | **Simple Green** sprayed onto a rag, then wipe | Don't use it on polycarbonate you want to stay clear — it clouds the appearance |
| Polycarbonate, Lexan and most plastics | Dish soap (Dawn/Joy/Palmolive) and water — about 3 tbsp in a spray bottle of water | Don't use solvents or Simple Green on clear parts |
| Fast-evaporating wipe-down, no residue | Isopropyl alcohol | — |
| Getting into corners and around gear teeth | Dollar-store toothbrush or paint brush, with a little Simple Green on the tip | — |
| Wheels and rollers before a match | Simple Green + microfibre; Q-tips for omni/mecanum rollers | Wipe them down before **every** match — it is free traction |
| Electronics, SB50s, breaker contacts, PDP/PDH breaker slots | **CRC Plastic Safe Contact Cleaner** (the shop's pick — plastic-safe, so it is also the one to use on silicone rollers, and it lifts general dust and debris so it wipes up easily). WD-40 Contact Cleaner also works. | Don't use a degreaser near the belly pan |
| Heavy grease work | Blue shop towels (stronger than paper towels, disposable) | — |
| Blowing a robot or machine clear | Shop compressed air at arm's length, goggles on | Never at skin, eyes or clothing; never on lathe ways |

### Lubrication

| Job | Use |
|---|---|
| Planetary gearboxes | **Tri-Flow** — a few drops on each gear |
| Chain and sprockets | **Tri-Flow** — a few drops on the sprockets |
| Gearbox interiors | **WD-40 Specialist Dry Lube with PTFE**, sprayed in while rotating the gearbox so every tooth gets coated |
| Lathe ways and leadscrew | ISO 68 way oil, light film at close |
| Tapping and threading steel | Dark sulfurized cutting oil |
| Tapping and drilling aluminum | WD-40 or a wax stick — but never dry |

### Threadlocker

<SafetyWarning type="warning" title="Never put threadlocker on polycarbonate, Lexan, ABS or acrylic">
Threadlocker chemically attacks those plastics and cracks them — sometimes days later, which makes it a miserable failure to diagnose. **Nylon and Delrin (acetal) are fine.** Every bolt that isn't secured by a nylon lock nut should get threadlocker, *unless* it is near or threaded into a plastic that will degrade.
</SafetyWarning>

**Which threadlocker:** the shop's pick is **Vibra-Tite 125 removable medium-strength gel**, in medium and high strength — "by far our favourite thread locker" per the team's own shop purchase review. The gel is the whole point: it applies far more cleanly than the liquid drip bottles and doesn't dry out like the glue-stick applicator. Loctite blue works and there is some on the shelf; the Vibra-Tite gel is what to reach for.

## Reporting & logging

Maintenance only works if problems get written down where someone will see them:

- **Found an issue?** Report it in the team's Slack (the shop channel) or tell the shop captain directly — same day, while you remember the details.
- **Machine unsafe or broken?** Power it down, put a clearly-written "DO NOT USE" note on it, and then report it. Never leave a fault for the next person to discover the hard way.
- **Completed a weekly/monthly task?** Say so in the same channel so it isn't done twice — or missed entirely because everyone assumed someone else did it.
- Batteries that won't charge, tools that are dull or rounded, and consumables running low all count as reports too — see [flagging low stock](/inventory/consumables/).
